\section{Quadratic match against a lifted zero}
\label{sec:h-j3w}

The \(J_3\) Hessian at the band bottom is \(4I_3\).
A chain of \(J_2\) sheets with transverse scale \(t_\perp\) has
\(\mathrm{Hess}=\mathrm{diag}(4,4,t_\perp)\).
The coordinate change \(z'=z\sqrt{t_\perp/4}\) identifies the quadratic forms
and leaves the quartic ratio of Theorem~\ref{thm:ratio} equal to \(0\) in the
plane, with a different transverse quartic.
The \(J_3\) flat band is the zero eigenvalue of
\(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\) at every crystal momentum.
On \(J_2\) the same matrix is Lemma~\ref{lem:sectors}: every
sheet-odd state is an exact zero mode, and the even sector carries
\(-4(\cos q_x+\cos q_y)\).
One stitch does not turn those zeros into a pair near \(\pm 1/2\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:stitch} puts the only eigenvalues outside \([-8,8]\)
at the roots of \(\mathcal{G}(E)=\pm 1\), next to the edges \(\pm 8\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:stubs} keeps every odd state that vanishes at the
stub cell.
A disjoint union of \(S\) sheets starts with \(S L^2\) flat zeros.
After the stitches the surviving zero count is the number of odd functions
that vanish on the stub cells of their sheet, which is not the \(J_3\)
count \(L^3\) plus touchings.
Section~\ref{sec:ell} is the metric form of the same \(t_\perp\), read as
\(\ell_W\) rather than as a hopping.
The same stitch on \(J_3\) is Proposition~\ref{prop:j3stitch}.
The odd kernel is Lemma~\ref{lem:j3sectors}, of dimension \(L^3\) on the
torus, and the edge of the even band is \(\pm 12\).
The resolvent stays finite at that edge and is strictly less than
\(11/48\), so the secular equation \(\mathcal{G}_3(E)^2=1\) has no root
outside \([-12,12]\).
The two dimensions removed from the kernel are absorbed by the continuum.
On \(J_2\) the two-dimensional edge sends the resolvent to infinity and
forces the pair \(\pm E_*\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint} saturates the stitch bond of those states
and gives the endpoint weight \(8\pi\) times the distance to the edge.
For hopping \(t\to 0\), Proposition~\ref{prop:weak} turns that product into
\(512\pi e^{\pi} t^{-2}\exp(-16\pi/|t|)\).
The same conclusion holds for every stitch hopping \(|t|\le 48/11\),
which includes the hopping of the sheet itself.
Proposition~\ref{prop:cellpole} is the resolvent that enters a weave with
several stubs on one sheet: the flat pole does not couple distinct cells.
An edge of the same hopping between the two bits of one cell is not that
stitch.
Proposition~\ref{prop:rung} keeps the odd delta of the cell as an exact
eigenstate at energy equal to the hopping, on \(J_2\) and on \(J_3\).
The even binding is one-sided.
On \(J_3\) every \(|t|\le 8/3\), including the hopping of the sheet, produces
no even eigenvalue outside \([-12,12]\), while on \(J_2\) every \(t\neq 0\)
produces one.
Two equal stitches into the two bits of one cell open an even channel and
an odd channel (Proposition~\ref{prop:twobit}).
On \(J_2\) each channel binds a pair outside \([-8,8]\).
On \(J_3\) every hopping \(|t|\le\sqrt{128/11}\), again including the sheet
hopping, binds nothing outside \([-12,12]\).
Adjacent cells, and any two distinct cells, carry no flat pole between
them.
Proposition~\ref{prop:adj} finds four eigenvalues outside \([-8,8]\)
on \(J_2\) when the cells are adjacent.
Proposition~\ref{prop:anysep} extends the existence to every nonzero
separation: the logarithm in the even resolvent is independent of that
separation.
The cancelled channel is not: Proposition~\ref{prop:logsep} evaluates
\[
\lambda_\infty(r)
=\frac1{8\pi}\log|r|
+\frac1{16}
+\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}+\frac32\log 2}{8\pi}
+o(1),
\]
where \(\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}=-\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\log u\,du\).
On the first shells the same edge value is elementary
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mean}): \(1/8\) at a neighbour,
\(1/16+1/(4\pi)\) on the diagonal, \(5/16-1/(2\pi)\) at separation \((2,0)\),
and \(1/(2\pi)\) at separation \((2,1)\).
On \(J_3\) both channels stay finite at every separation.
Proposition~\ref{prop:j3max} shows that the larger edge product is
maximised when the cells are adjacent, where one channel equals \(1/12\)
and the other equals \(I/4\).
Every non-adjacent pair has a strictly higher critical hopping.
Every \(|t|\le\sqrt{128/11}\), including the sheet hopping, binds nothing
outside \([-12,12]\), at every separation.
