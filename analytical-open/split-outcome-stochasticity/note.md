# The continuous fiber coordinate is fixed by \(B\), or it is Haar, or it is stored

H2 asks whether a decay that is not replaying \(Q\) has a random daughter.
The algebra in `hidden-store-entropy/note.md` removes part of that
question.

The daughter data are \((c,d)\) with \(d\in\mathbb C\). The combination
that enters the isolated information is only

\[
P_-=\frac12-\frac{B}{q},
\]

a single real function of the pair. The phase of \(d\) at \(B=0\) is the
current and is not fixed by \(P_-\). So "which \(\xi\)" splits into:

- the cover \(c\), discrete;
- the bond \(B\), or equivalently \(P_-\);
- the current (phase of \(d\)).

**Stored \(Q\).** All three are fixed. Products are deterministic. This is
earned (STORE0).

**Haar pair, \(\xi\) not stored.** \(P_-\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and
\(\langle h_2\rangle=1/(2\ln 2)\). The daughter weight is random, with a
known distribution, not with an unknown measure. FIBER0's 520-dimensional
measure debt is about a normalized measure on the whole fiber that has to
satisfy every earned constraint. The marginal of \(P_-\) for a Haar state
is already uniform, with no free parameter. A selection law is still
needed if the physical split is **not** a Haar draw. It is not needed to
know what a Haar draw would do.

**Scale-separated or \(B=0\) pair.** \(P_-\to 1/2\) kinematically. The
weight is then deterministic. The phase remains free unless the current is
fixed by the jet or by \(Q\).

A campaign that estimates a selection measure on \(d\in\mathbb C\) before
classifying the parent as stored, Haar, or scale-separated will fit a
mixture of these three laws and call it a result. Classify the parent
first. The histogram of \(P_-\) on the split pair is the classifier: a
delta at \(1/2\), a delta at \(0\), or a flat line on \([0,1]\).
