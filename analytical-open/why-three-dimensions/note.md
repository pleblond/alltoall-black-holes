# Three is the first dimension whose band edge can host a long-lived shallow mode

P0' imports Maxwell dimension \(2\) when it sets \(\langle z\rangle=4\). The
graph alone does not force that integer: honeycomb, square, and triangular
lattices are all two-dimensional at different coordination (relaxed-vacuum
essay). The weave formula

\[
d_{\mathrm{obs}}=d_{\mathrm{sheet}}+d_M
\]

gives \(3\) when the sheet is \(2\) and the meeting complex is
one-dimensional. That is a minimality statement (smallest surface, smallest
meeting dimension that adds one noncompact direction). It is not a
stability theorem. The missing firing law is what would make it one.

A separate spectral fact uses only the earned quadratic symbol. Near a band
edge with Hessian proportional to the identity, the density of states in
\(d\) dimensions behaves as

\[
\rho(E)\propto
\begin{cases}
(E-E_0)^{-1/2} & d=1,\\
\mathrm{const} & d=2,\\
(E-E_0)^{1/2} & d=3,\\
(E-E_0)^{d/2-1} & d\ge 2.
\end{cases}
\]

J2's dispersive edge is the \(d=2\) case, \(\rho\to 1/(8\pi)\) per quotient
cell at \(J=1\) because \(m^*=1/4\) and
\(\int d^2q/(2\pi)^2\,\delta(q^2/(2m)-E)=m/(2\pi)\). J3's edge is the
\(d=3\) case.

Fermi's golden rule for a resonance coupled to that continuum,
\(\Gamma=2\pi|V|^2\rho(E)\), stays finite as \(E\downarrow E_0\) in two
dimensions and vanishes as \(\sqrt{E-E_0}\) in three. Shallow radiative
lifetimes can be parametrically long only for \(d\ge 3\). Dimension \(3\) is
the lowest weave lift of a \(2\)-dimensional sheet and, independently, the
lowest dimension with a vanishing band-edge density of states. A test that
compares resonance widths of a weakly coupled defect on J2 against the same
defect on J3 is a direct check of this factor. It does not require the
weave to be built, and it does not require a cosmological selection
argument.

Higher meeting dimension \(d_M\ge 2\) would give \(d_{\mathrm{obs}}\ge 4\),
which also has vanishing edge density of states. The weave minimality
argument, not the density of states, is what stops at \(3\). Both have to
be stated; the density of states alone does not forbid \(4\).
