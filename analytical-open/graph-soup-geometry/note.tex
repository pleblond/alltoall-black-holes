\section{Cycle density between a tree and an expander}
\label{sec:h-soup}

A sparse tree has exponential ball growth,
\(V(r)\sim (z-1)^r\), until the finite size cuts it off.
The spectral dimension of a critical branched polymer is \(4/3\).
There is no integer plateau of \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\).
An Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graph above the connectivity threshold, or a
configuration model with excess degree and large girth, is an expander:
the diameter is \(\sim\log N\), and a transport estimator that assumes a
polynomial ball does not return a stable embedding dimension.

The earned geometric graphs sit between these.
\(J_2\) and the square have a density of \(4\)-cycles of order one per
vertex.
The chain weave adds a thin set of stitches and keeps the sheet cycles.
The fraction \(f_\square\) of edges that lie in a \(4\)-cycle is \(0\) on a
tree, of order one on the square and on \(J_2\), and small on a dilute
random regular graph of large girth.

Across a percolation of those cycles at fixed degree, a logarithmic
derivative that was large on the tree side falls toward \(2\).
A later weave window at \(3\) is Section~\ref{sec:ell}, and it is
intermediate when \(S\) is finite.
You could plot \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\) against \(f_\square\) on one
configuration-model family as short cycles are added, with the degree
sequence held fixed.
The expander side, \(f_\square\to 0\) at high degree, does not pass
through a plateau at \(3\) on that path.
Section~\ref{sec:soup} is this coordinate written as the order parameter
of the soup.
