\section{Volume window of a chain of sheets}
\label{sec:h-weave}

Sheets of intrinsic area growth \(r^2\), meeting graph of dimension
\(d_M\), and crossover \(\ell_W\) give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Vchain}
V(r)\sim
\begin{cases}
r^2 & r\ll\ell_W,\\
(r/\ell_W)^{d_M}\,r^2 & \ell_W\ll r\ll R_\perp,\\
S\,r^2 & r\gg R_\perp,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with \(R_\perp=S\ell_W\) when \(d_M=1\) and the meeting graph is a ring of
\(S\) sheets.
In that case the middle regime is \(V\sim r^3/\ell_W\).

On an infinite line of sheets the middle regime is the infrared:
\(V\sim r^3/\ell_W\) for every \(r\gg\ell_W\).
The return to thick two-dimensional growth is the finite-\(S\)
compactification, circumference \(S\ell_W\).
A finite ring that shows the exponent \(3\) only inside a window has
exhibited \eqref{eq:Vchain}.
Absence of the re-entrant tail on that ring means the probe has not
reached \(r\sim S\ell_W\).

A dense meeting graph, \(d_M=\infty\), has no middle regime:
\(V\sim S r^2\) as soon as the stitches are felt.
A \(3\)-regular random meeting graph is locally a tree.
Its volume is exponential in graph distance and has no stable power \(3\).

The exponent \(3\) and the axis ratio \(\ell_W\) are independent readings of
\eqref{eq:ds}.
An ellipsoid has the same volume exponent as a ball.
Section~\ref{sec:h-j3w} is the spectral form of the same distinction:
a quadratic packet, after a transverse rescaling, does not see the
lifted sheet zero.
