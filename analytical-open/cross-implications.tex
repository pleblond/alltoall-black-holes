\section{One bond number}
\label{sec:bond}

\subsection{The identities}

Let \(\psi_i,\psi_j\in\C\) and write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Bq}
B_{ij}=\Re(\psi_i^*\psi_j),\qquad
q_{ij}=|\psi_i|^2+|\psi_j|^2,\qquad
s=\psi_i+\psi_j,\qquad
d=\psi_i-\psi_j.
\end{equation}
The sum-map convention in \texttt{split0.fiber\_point} is the same \(s\) and \(d\),
up to which daughter is called \(i\).
The swap \(d\mapsto -d\) is the endpoint gauge already quotiented by STORE.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:bond-expand}
\begin{align*}
|s|^2&=q_{ij}+2B_{ij},&
|d|^2&=q_{ij}-2B_{ij},\\
|s|^2-|d|^2&=4B_{ij},&
|s|^2+|d|^2&=2q_{ij}.
\end{align*}
If \(q_{ij}>0\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Pminus}
P_-=\frac{|d|^2}{|s|^2+|d|^2}
=\frac12-\frac{B_{ij}}{q_{ij}},\qquad
P_+=\frac12+\frac{B_{ij}}{q_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
Furthermore \(|B_{ij}|\le q_{ij}/2\), with equality if and only if
\(\psi_i\) and \(\psi_j\) are nonnegative real multiples of one complex phase.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand \(|\psi_i\pm\psi_j|^2\).
The cross term is \(2\Re(\psi_i^*\psi_j)\).
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the bound on \(B_{ij}\).
The unitary modes \(\alpha=s/\sqrt{2}\) and \(\beta=d/\sqrt{2}\)
produce the same ratio \(P_-\), because the factors of \(2\) cancel.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:Bzero}
For \(q_{ij}>0\),
\[
B_{ij}=0
\iff |s|=|d|
\iff P_+=P_-=\tfrac12.
\]
The sum-map norm change is \(\Delta\|\psi\|^2=2B_{ij}\)
(\texttt{accounting.event\_ledger}).
Hence \(B_{ij}=0\) if and only if that contraction is norm-neutral.
The energy change is
\(\Delta E_\psi=2B_{ij}-2\Sigma_{\mathrm{cross}}\).
On \(K_N\) every third vertex is a common neighbour, so
\(\Sigma_{\mathrm{cross}}=0\) and norm neutrality coincides with energy neutrality.
On a cut leg the exclusive-neighbour sum survives, and the two neutralities diverge.
\end{corollary}

If \(q_{ij}=0\) both amplitudes vanish.
There is then no pair, and \(P_-\) is undefined.
The limit of \(h_2(P_-)\) along \(q\to 0\) depends on the direction of approach,
so an empty pair is not assigned the value \(1\) by continuity.

\subsection{Binary entropy, as an optional reading}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:h2}
INFO-0 recorded counts with binary logarithms and did not introduce
\(-\sum p\log p\).
The map \(B/q\mapsto P_-\) does not need a further identification.
You could still attach to an occupied pair the Shannon entropy of its
\(\pm\) weight,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:se}
s_e=h_2(P_-),\qquad
h_2(p)=-p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p).
\end{equation}
Then \(0\le s_e\le 1\), with \(s_e=1\) if and only if \(B_{ij}=0\),
and \(s_e=0\) at the Cauchy--Schwarz ends.
This \(s_e\) is the uncertainty of one projective \(\pm\) measurement.
It is not the von Neumann entropy of a pure global state.
It does not see the phase of \(d\) relative to \(s\).
At \(B_{ij}=0\) that phase is a pure current:
\(|\Im(\psi_i^*\psi_j)|=|\psi_i||\psi_j|\).
The continuous fibre dimension \(\dim_\R d=2\) counts magnitude and phase.
The number \(s_e\) counts neither as a dimension.
The two are not proportional, which is the overlap between the
post-STORE entropy row and the BH-Q dimension count
(Section~\ref{sec:kernel}).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:taylor}
With \(x=B/q\) and \(|x|\le 1/2\),
\[
h_2\Bigl(\tfrac12-x\Bigr)
=1-\frac{2}{\ln 2}\,x^2-\frac{4}{3\ln 2}\,x^4+O(x^6).
\]
There is no odd term.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set \(f(x)=(\tfrac12-x)\ln(\tfrac12-x)+(\tfrac12+x)\ln(\tfrac12+x)\),
so \(h_2=-f/\ln 2\).
Then \(f(0)=-\ln 2\), \(f'(0)=0\),
\(f''(x)=4/(1-4x^2)\), \(f''(0)=4\), \(f'''(0)=0\), \(f''''(0)=32\).
The degree-\(4\) Taylor polynomial is \(-\ln 2+2x^2+\tfrac43 x^4\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Three ensembles, one pair}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:haar}
Let \(\psi\) be Haar-uniform on the unit sphere in \(\C^N\), \(N\ge 2\),
and let \(\{i,j\}\) be any fixed pair.
Then \(P_-\) is uniform on \([0,1]\), and
\[
\mathbb{E}[h_2(P_-)]=\frac{1}{2\ln 2}.
\]
The expectation is exact at finite \(N\), not a large-\(N\) limit.
Weighting pairs by \(q_{ij}\) does not change it:
\(h_2(P_-)\) depends only on the fraction, and for the Dirichlet law
the fraction is independent of the pair norm.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The squared moduli of a Haar vector in \(\C^N\) are Dirichlet with every
parameter equal to \(1\).
They have the law of \((e_1,\ldots,e_N)/\sum_k e_k\) with \(e_k\) i.i.d.\
exponential.
For any two coordinates,
\(e_i/(e_i+e_j)\) is uniform on \([0,1]\).
The rotation
\(\alpha=(\psi_i+\psi_j)/\sqrt{2}\),
\(\beta=(\psi_i-\psi_j)/\sqrt{2}\)
is a global unitary.
Haar measure is invariant, so
\(P_-=|\beta|^2/(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)\) is uniform on \([0,1]\).
The integral \(\int_0^1 -p\ln p\,dp=1/4\), and the partner term in \(p\mapsto 1-p\)
is equal, so
\[
\int_0^1 h_2(p)\,dp=\frac{1}{\ln 2}\cdot\frac12=\frac{1}{2\ln 2}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:arcsine}
If the two moduli are equal and the relative phase is uniform,
or if the pair is a real Gaussian,
then \(P_-\) is Beta\(\bigl(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\bigr)\) and
\[
\mathbb{E}[h_2(P_-)]=2-\frac{1}{\ln 2}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equal moduli and uniform phase give
\(P_-=(1-\cos\phi)/2\).
The density of this law is \(1/(\pi\sqrt{p(1-p)})\).
A real Gaussian pair has the same norm fraction, because the argument of
\((g_1,g_2)\) is uniform.
One of the two symmetric terms in the average is
\[
\frac{1}{\pi\ln 2}\int_0^1\frac{-p\ln p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}\,dp
=\frac{1}{\pi\ln 2}\bigl(-\partial_a B(a,b)\bigr)_{a=3/2,\,b=1/2}.
\]
Here \(B(3/2,1/2)=\pi/2\) and \(\psi(3/2)-\psi(2)=1-2\ln 2\),
so \(-\partial_a B=(\pi/2)(2\ln 2-1)\).
One term equals \((2\ln 2-1)/(2\ln 2)\).
Both terms give \(2-1/\ln 2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:ground}
The normalised all-ones vector on \(K_N\), which is the ground state of
\(H=-A\), has \(B_{ij}/q_{ij}=1/2\) on every edge, hence \(P_-=0\) and \(h_2=0\).
\end{proposition}

A flat-magnitude random-phase vector is Proposition~\ref{prop:arcsine},
not Theorem~\ref{thm:haar}.
The two means differ:
\(1/(2\ln 2)\approx 0.721\) against \(2-1/\ln 2\approx 0.557\).

\subsection{Scale separation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:lambda}
Fix \(v>0\) and let \(\psi_i=\sigma g\) with \(g\) complex normal,
\(\mathbb{E}|g|^2=1\), and \(\psi_j=v\) real.
Set \(\lambda=\sigma/v\). Then
\[
x=\frac{B}{q}=\frac{\lambda\,\Re g}{1+\lambda^2|g|^2}.
\]
As \(\lambda\to 0\) or \(\lambda\to\infty\), \(x\to 0\) in probability,
so \(h_2(1/2-x)\to 1\) in probability.
For small \(\lambda\), \(\mathbb{E}[(\Re g)^2]=1/2\) and
\[
1-\mathbb{E}[h_2]=\frac{\lambda^2}{\ln 2}+O(\lambda^4).
\]
\end{proposition}

You could read this as follows.
A dynamical selection of quadrature is one way to reach \(h_2=1\).
A mismatch of the two amplitudes is another, and it does not use the
phase. The case \(\psi_j=0\), \(\psi_i\neq 0\) is exactly \(B=0\).
That endpoint is the \(\psi=0\) limit, which the ledger already separates
from the JOINT vacua.

\subsection{Where the same number sits}

I1a assigns \(\ln 2\) nats, hence one bit, to a saturated leg, and the
matching theorem gives \(A=4\ln 2\cdot k\,\ell_P^2\).
In bits, \(S=k\) and
\[
\frac{S}{A}=\frac{1}{4\ell_P^2\ln 2}.
\]
You could take the channel set to be the \(k\) cut legs and set
\(S_Q=\sum_e h_2(P_{-,e})\).
Then \(S_Q=k\) if and only if \(\bar h=1\),
and the coefficient above is reproduced with no free area.
A Haar law on those same legs would give \(S_Q=k/(2\ln 2)\) instead,
short by the constant \(2\ln 2\).
The interior edges of \(K_N\) are a different set:
there are \(\binom{N}{2}\) of them, and Theorem~\ref{thm:haar}
puts about \(0.721\) bit on each.
Edges of \(K_N\) incident to a leg-bearing vertex number about \(kN\).
The cardinality of the set is part of the definition of the sum.
The algebra does not choose the set.

BR2-QUADRATURE says that structural response tracks \(\cos\Delta\theta\)
through \(B\) and a staggered flux tracks \(\sin\Delta\theta\) through the current.
It does not set \(B_{ij}=0\).
The word ``quadrature'' in that verdict and the condition \(B=0\)
share a trigonometric function and not a theorem.

\section{Kernel dimension is a different count}
\label{sec:kernel}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:fiber}
The sum map \((p,q)\mapsto s=p+q\) has kernel the complex line \(d=p-q\),
real dimension \(2\), for every \(s\) including \(s=0\).
After the \(U(1)\) quotient the physical dimension drops to \(1\) only for
the all-zero merged state.
The swap \(d\mapsto -d\) is discrete and does not halve the dimension.
\end{lemma}

A tree of \(n-1\) interior merges therefore carries naive continuous
dimension \(2(n-1)\), minus all-zero exceptions.
That is the count \(D_Q=2N_d+D_c\) in the ledger.
Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber} and Remark~\ref{rem:h2} do not convert it into \(h_2\).

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:vol}
Suppose each merge contributes an independent complex coordinate,
and the exterior imprint of those coordinates factors through a complex
boundary space of dimension at most \(b=|\partial R|\).
Then
\[
D_Q\ge 2(N_Q-b).
\]
For a space-filling collapse, \(N_Q=n_R-1\), so the lower bound is
volume-leading.
If the exterior evolution is the collapsed graph alone, \(Q\) does not
enter \(H\) (the store is frozen between events), the Jacobian on the
\(d\)-coordinates vanishes, and \(D_Q=2(n_R-1)\) up to all-zero exceptions.
\end{proposition}

An area law for this kernel would need either \(N_Q\) itself of order \(b\),
or an imprint whose rank cancels the bulk and leaves a boundary layer.
Neither is implied by \(H=-A\) or by the sum map.
You could have a volume law for \(D_Q\) and, on a different set of edges,
an area law for \(\sum h_2\).
They count different things: interior relative modes, and leg channels.
Promoting the factor \(2\) in \(D_Q=2N_d\) to two bits per merge
is a third count, distinct from I1a's one bit per saturated leg.

\section{One length in the chain weave}
\label{sec:ell}

The chain construction places stitch endpoints at density \(\rho=4\lambda\)
on the sheets and sets
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ell}
\ell_W=\rho^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}
\end{equation}
in sheet cells.
A transverse step costs an in-plane walk of that typical length, plus one
stitch edge.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:metric}
In coordinates \((x,y,z)\) with \(z\) the sheet index, the Riemannian
approximation consistent with that cost is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ds}
ds^2=dx^2+dy^2+\ell_W^2\,dz^2.
\end{equation}
The linear axis ratio is \(\ell_W\).
In-plane shifts and a transpose act on \((x,y)\).
They do not rotate \(z\) into the sheet.
The quadratic symbol of \(J_2\) is already isotropic in the sheet
(\(\mathrm{Hess}=4I\)), so those moves have no quadratic in-plane
anisotropy left to erase.
\end{proposition}

Volume growth in the window \(\ell_W\ll r\ll S\ell_W\) is
\(V\sim r^3/\ell_W\) for a one-dimensional meeting graph.
An ellipsoid has the same volume exponent as a ball.
The exponent \(3\) and the ratio \(\ell_W\) are independent readings of
\eqref{eq:ds}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:over}
\(\ell_W=1\) forces \(\lambda=1/4\).
The stub fraction \(1-\exp(-2\lambda)\) is then \(1-e^{-1/2}\approx 0.393\),
which the weave preregistration labels overconnected.
Every woven ladder value \(\lambda\le 0.08\) has \(\ell_W\ge 1.77\).
\end{proposition}

The same \(\ell_W\) is the crossover of \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\) and,
if stitches are later removed, the transverse factor in any scale
read off \eqref{eq:ds}.
A scalar \(a\propto 1/\rho_S\) built from this metric tracks \(a_\perp\),
not an isotropic scale factor.
The in-plane factor stays \(1\) while the sheet lattice is held fixed.
The ledger average \(M_{ab}=\langle n_a n_b\rangle\) needs normals in an
ambient three-space.
The chain construction does not have those normals.
An isotropic plane process would be a different ensemble, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:metric} would not apply to it.

On a finite ring the third direction is compact, circumference \(S\ell_W\).
For \(S=\infty\) the window is the infrared and the axis ratio remains.
Spectral dimension of the homogenized walk is \(3\) only inside the time
window corresponding to \eqref{eq:ell}, and returns to \(2\) after the ring
is explored.
That return is the compactification, not a second mechanism.

\section{One quartic ratio}
\label{sec:quartic}

At \(J=1\),
\begin{align}
\label{eq:disp}
\varepsilon_{J_2}(q)
&=-8+2|q|^2-\tfrac16(q_x^4+q_y^4)+O(q^6),\\
\varepsilon_{J_3}(q)
&=-12+2|q|^2-\tfrac16\sum_i q_i^4+O(q^6).
\end{align}
Both are sums of functions of one momentum component, so every
coefficient of \(q_i^2 q_j^2\) vanishes.
The Hessian at the band bottom is \(4I\).

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:ratio}
Write the quartic part as
\(\beta\sum_i q_i^4+\gamma\sum_{i<j}q_i^2 q_j^2\).
A quartic function of \(|q|^2\), and the degree-\(4\) term in
\(\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2p^2}\), both require \(\gamma/\beta=2\).
For \eqref{eq:disp}, \(\beta=-1/6\) and \(\gamma=0\).
No choice of \(m\), \(c\), or \(J\) removes the mismatch.
The first rotationally anisotropic correction and the first departure from
the relativistic series sit at the same order.
\end{theorem}

The long-wave ray direction can still be radial.
On \(J_2\),
\(v_i=4q_i-(2/3)q_i^3+\cdots\), so \(v\) is parallel to \(q\) on the axes
and the diagonals, and the deflection elsewhere is \(O(q^2)\).
The Manhattan cone is a different object: for
\(\varepsilon=-4\sum_{i=1}^d\cos q_i\),
\[
\frac{v_E^{\max}}{v_M}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}},
\]
equal to \(1/\sqrt{2}\) on \(J_2\) and \(1/\sqrt{3}\) on \(J_3\).
A threshold on the peak and a threshold in the forerunner read these two
speeds. The ratio does not require a continuum fit.

A chain weave has Hessian \(\mathrm{diag}(4,4,t_\perp)\) at quadratic order.
Rescaling the sheet-index coordinate matches this to \(4I_3\) and does not
match the quartic, nor the flat band.
The \(J_3\) zero eigenvalue is structural at every crystal momentum.
A stitch with \(\|P_- H P_+\|\neq 0\) lifts the corresponding sheet zero.
Nullity against stitch density is the spectral distinction;
a quadratic packet, after the rescaling, does not see it.

Dimension universality in the earned sense is universality of the
quadratic form: SCALE-0 has \(d_H\to 2\) with \(J_2\) and the square control
indistinguishable.
Fringes at a few cells see \(O(q^2)\) corrections and the plaquette.
Two graphs with the same Hessian and different \(\gamma/\beta\)
agree on \(d_H\) and need not agree on interference.
That is why a spectral feature vector can fail to classify F
while still classifying the volume exponent.

\section{Band edge, dimension, and a gap}
\label{sec:dos}

Near a band edge whose Hessian is a multiple of the identity,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dos}
\rho(E)\propto
\begin{cases}
(E-E_0)^{-1/2}& d=1,\\
\mathrm{const}& d=2,\\
(E-E_0)^{d/2-1}& d\ge 2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The \(J_2\) dispersive edge is the \(d=2\) case.
With \(m^*=1/4\) and \(J=1\),
\(\rho\to m^*/(2\pi)=1/(8\pi)\) per quotient cell.
The \(J_3\) edge is the \(d=3\) case, vanishing as \(\sqrt{E-E_0}\).

Fermi's golden-rule width \(\Gamma=2\pi|V|^2\rho(E)\) therefore stays finite
as \(E\downarrow E_0\) on \(J_2\) and can be parametrically small on \(J_3\).
Dimension \(3\) is the lowest dimension in \eqref{eq:dos} with a vanishing
edge density, and it is also the weave value
\(d_{\mathrm{obs}}=d_{\mathrm{sheet}}+d_M\) for a \(2\)-dimensional sheet
and a \(1\)-dimensional meeting complex.
The density of states does not forbid \(d\ge 4\).
The weave count does, and only as a minimality reading:
P0' imports Maxwell dimension \(2\) when it sets \(\langle z\rangle=4\),
and the graph alone does not force that integer.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:clique}
On infinite \(J_2\) the essential spectrum of \(H=-A\) is \([-8,8]\) together
with the flat band at \(0\).
A finite edge perturbation does not move the essential spectrum.
If a vertex subset induces a clique \(K_n\) with \(n\ge 10\),
the normalised indicator of that subset has Rayleigh quotient \(-(n-1)\)
for \(H\), so
\[
E_0\le -(n-1)\le -9,\qquad m=-8-E_0\ge n-9.
\]
\end{proposition}

For \(n\le 9\) this trial vector does not clear the edge.
A triangle scores \(-2\), inside the band.
Modes inside the essential spectrum hybridize with \eqref{eq:dos}.
You could regard a motif with no eigenvalue below the edge as a resonance
of the earned unitary, and a motif with such an eigenvalue as a spectral
bound state.
The flat band on pure \(J_2\) is a protected kernel, extensive in the
sheet-antisymmetric sector, and it does not propagate in the quotient.
What one stitch does to that kernel is Section~\ref{sec:stitch}.

The event account \(R=A_{\mathrm{coef}}+|d|^2/2+\Re(\bar d\, W)\)
telescopes along a sequence of merges to the change in known energy.
Between events \(E_\psi\) is conserved.
Q-DYN-0b treats \(R\) as event-local, not as a term stored in \(Q\).
The spectral gap of Proposition~\ref{prop:clique} depends only on the
final graph and the final eigenmode.
Two histories that reach the same \((G,\psi)\) share that gap and need not
share \(\sum R\).

At fixed \(\psi\), an edge-set change shifts \(E_\psi\) only through
\(-2\sum(\Delta A_e) B_e\).
There is no extra term in \(H=-A\) coupling to \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\).
Deleting stitches on which \(B_e=0\) changes ball growth and does not change
\(E_\psi\).
A mass read from the eigenvalue and a dimension read from the ball are
independent at that locus.

Isolated \(K_n\) has spectrum \(\{-(n-1)\}\) once and \(\{+1\}\) with
multiplicity \(n-1\).
Only the ground level can sit below \(-8\), and only for \(n\ge 10\).
The level \(+1\) lies in \([-8,8]\).

\section{One stitch and the flat band}
\label{sec:stitch}

The eight generators of \(J_2\) are the axis steps
\((\pm 1,0)\) and \((0,\pm 1)\), each once with the sheet bit fixed and
once with the sheet bit flipped.
On the sheet with bit \(1\) the axes in the group law are swapped.
At hopping \(1\), \(H=-A\).

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:sectors}
If \(\psi(x,y,1)=-\psi(x,y,0)\) at every cell, then \(A\psi=0\).
If \(\psi(x,y,1)=\psi(x,y,0)=\varphi(x,y)\), then
\[
(A\psi)(x,y,b)=2(A_\square\varphi)(x,y)
\]
at both values of the bit, where \(A_\square\) is the adjacency of the
square lattice.
The eigenvalues of \(H\) on the even Bloch waves are therefore
\(-4(\cos q_x+\cos q_y)\), and every odd state is a zero mode.
On the \(L\)-torus the odd space has dimension \(L^2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At bit \(0\) the eight neighbours of \((x,y)\) are the four adjacent cells
on bit \(0\) and the same four cells on bit \(1\).
An odd state assigns opposite amplitudes to those two bits, so the eight
terms cancel in pairs.
At bit \(1\) the swapped axes still reach exactly the same four adjacent
cells, on each bit, and the same cancellation applies.
An even state assigns the same amplitude \(\varphi\) on both bits, so each
pair of neighbours contributes \(2\varphi\), which is \(2(A_\square\varphi)\).
The symbol of \(A_\square\) is \(2(\cos q_x+\cos q_y)\), and \(H=-A\)
contributes a further minus sign together with that factor \(2\).
The odd space is a free function of the cell, hence dimension \(L^2\).
\end{proof}

A stitch is one added edge of hopping \(1\) between a vertex \(a\) on one
copy of this graph and a vertex \(b\) on another copy.
The perturbation of \(H\) is
\(V=-(|a\rangle\langle b|+|b\rangle\langle a|)\).
The two copies do not meet in \(H_0\), so the resolvent of \(H_0\) is
diagonal across copies.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:green}
For \(E\notin[-8,8]\) the diagonal resolvent entry of one copy is independent
of the vertex.
For \(E>8\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gell}
\mathcal{G}(E)
=\frac{1}{2E}+\frac{1}{\pi E}\,K\Bigl(\frac{8}{E}\Bigr),
\qquad
K(k)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\theta}}.
\end{equation}
The function is odd, \(\mathcal{G}(-E)=-\mathcal{G}(E)\), wherever both sides are defined.
On \((8,\infty)\) it is strictly decreasing, from \(+\infty\) down to \(0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the Bloch basis of Lemma~\ref{lem:sectors} the sheet block at momentum
\(q\) has eigenvalues \(0\) and \(\varepsilon(q)=-4(\cos q_x+\cos q_y)\),
with equal diagonal weight \(1/2\) in either bit.
Hence
\[
\mathcal{G}(E)=\frac{1}{2E}
+\frac12\int_{[0,2\pi]^2}\frac{d^2q}{(2\pi)^2}\,
\frac{1}{E-\varepsilon(q)},
\]
for \(E\) outside the range of \(\varepsilon\), which is \([-8,8]\).
The shift \(q\mapsto q+(\pi,\pi)\) sends \(\varepsilon\) to \(-\varepsilon\) and
preserves the measure, so the integral is an odd function of \(E\), and
so is \(1/(2E)\).

For the closed form, write \(E-\varepsilon(q)=E+4(\cos q_x+\cos q_y)\) and
\[
\int\frac{d^2q}{(2\pi)^2}\,\frac{1}{E-\varepsilon(q)}
=\frac14\int\frac{d^2q}{(2\pi)^2}\,
\frac{1}{a+\cos q_x+\cos q_y},
\qquad a=\frac{E}{4}>2.
\]
Shifting both angles by \(\pi\) replaces the denominator by
\(a-\cos q_x-\cos q_y\).
The inner average is a contour integral.
With \(z=e^{i\phi}\),
\[
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{b-\cos\phi}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b^2-1}},\qquad b>1,
\]
because the integrand becomes
\(2i\,dz/((z-z_+)(z-z_-))\) with poles
\(z_\pm=b\pm\sqrt{b^2-1}\), only \(z_-\) inside the unit circle, and the
residue computation produces this value.
The condition \(b=a-\cos q_x>1\) holds for all \(q_x\) precisely when \(a>2\).
The momentum integral therefore equals
\[
J(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{dk}{\sqrt{(a-\cos k)^2-1}}
=\frac{2}{\pi a}\,K\Bigl(\frac{2}{a}\Bigr).
\]
The evaluation of \(J\) is the substitution \(t=\tan(k/2)\) on \((0,\pi)\),
followed by \(t=\sqrt{(a-2)/a}\,\tan\theta\).
It produces the modulus \(2/a\).
Since \(a=E/4\), one has \(2/a=8/E\), and
\[
\frac12\cdot\frac14\cdot J(a)
=\frac{1}{\pi E}\,K\Bigl(\frac{8}{E}\Bigr).
\]
Adding the flat pole \(1/(2E)\) gives \eqref{eq:Gell}.

Each summand \(1/(E-\varepsilon(q))\) is strictly decreasing for \(E>8\), so
\(\mathcal{G}\) is strictly decreasing.
As \(E\to\infty\), \(\mathcal{G}(E)\to 0\).
As \(E\downarrow 8\), \(K(8/E)\to\infty\) because the integrand of \(K\)
ceases to be integrable at modulus \(1\), so \(\mathcal{G}(E)\to+\infty\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:stitch}
Let \(H=H_0+V\) be two copies joined by one stitch.
An energy \(E\notin[-8,8]\) is an eigenvalue if and only if \(\mathcal{G}(E)^2=1\).
There is a unique \(E_*>8\) with \(G(E_*)=1\), and the partner \(-E_*\) is the
unique solution of \(\mathcal{G}(E)=-1\) below \(-8\).
These two eigenvalues lie outside the essential spectrum, which
Proposition~\ref{prop:clique} keeps equal to \([-8,8]\) together with the
flat band.
The distance above the edge admits the expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Estar}
\ln\frac{1}{E_*-8}=15\pi-6\ln 2-16\pi\, r(E_*-8),
\end{equation}
where \(r(\delta)\to 0\) as \(\delta\downarrow 0\), and \(r(E_*-8)\) is the
value of that remainder at the root.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalue equation \((E-H_0)\psi=V\psi\) gives
\[
\psi=-\mathcal{G}_0(E)\bigl(\psi(b)\,|a\rangle+\psi(a)\,|b\rangle\bigr).
\]
Off-diagonal blocks of \(\mathcal{G}_0\) between the copies vanish, so
\(\psi(a)=-\mathcal{G}(E)\psi(b)\) and \(\psi(b)=-\mathcal{G}(E)\psi(a)\).
A solution with \(\psi(a)\) or \(\psi(b)\) nonzero exists exactly when
\(\mathcal{G}(E)^2=1\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:green} supplies one root of \(G=1\) in \((8,\infty)\)
and, by oddness, one root of \(G=-1\) in \((-\infty,-8)\).
A finite-rank perturbation does not move the essential spectrum, so
\(\pm E_*\) are discrete.

For the logarithm, the representation
\[
K(k)=\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{\sqrt{(1+t^2)(1+k'^2 t^2)}},
\qquad k'=\sqrt{1-k^2},
\]
follows from \(t=\tan\theta\).
Equivalently, with \(u=k't\),
\[
K(k)=\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{\sqrt{(u^2+k'^2)(1+u^2)}}.
\]
Splitting at \(u=1\) gives the identity
\begin{align*}
K(k)-\ln\frac{2}{k'}
&=\int_0^1\frac{1}{\sqrt{u^2+k'^2}}
\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}-1\Bigr)\,du\\
&\quad+\int_1^\infty\frac{du}{\sqrt{(u^2+k'^2)(1+u^2)}}
+\ln\frac{1+\sqrt{1+k'^2}}{2},
\end{align*}
because
\[
\int_0^1\frac{du}{\sqrt{u^2+k'^2}}=\ln\frac{1+\sqrt{1+k'^2}}{k'}.
\]
As \(k'\to 0\) the logarithm tends to \(0\) and the integrands increase to
their pointwise limits, which are integrable, so
\[
K(k)-\ln\frac{2}{k'}\to
\int_0^1\frac1u\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}-1\Bigr)\,du
+\int_1^\infty\frac{du}{u\sqrt{1+u^2}}.
\]
Those two integrals sum to \(\ln 2\).
The first of those is \(\ln 2-\ln(1+\sqrt{2})\) after
\(w=u^2\) and \(\sqrt{1+w}=s\); the second is \(\operatorname{arsinh} 1=\ln(1+\sqrt{2})\).
Thus \(K(k)=\ln(4/k')+o(1)\) as \(k'\to 0\).

At \(E=8+\delta\) the modulus in \eqref{eq:Gell} is \(k=8/E\), so
\[
k'=\sqrt{1-\frac{64}{E^2}}
=\frac{\sqrt{\delta(16+\delta)}}{8+\delta}
\]
and
\[
\ln\frac{4}{k'}=\frac12\ln\frac1\delta+3\ln 2+\eta(\delta),
\]
with \(\eta(\delta)\to 0\) as \(\delta\downarrow 0\).
Dividing by \(\pi E\) and adding \(1/(2E)\) produces
\[
G(8+\delta)
=\frac{1}{16\pi}\ln\frac1\delta+\frac1{16}+\frac{3\ln 2}{8\pi}+r(\delta),
\]
where the remainder \(r(\delta)\) tends to \(0\) as \(\delta\downarrow 0\).
Rearrangement at the unique root of \(G=1\) is \eqref{eq:Estar}.
\end{proof}

The values \(\pm 1/2\) are the eigenvalues of the stitch projected onto the
odd sector: the normalised odd state has amplitude \(1/\sqrt{2}\) on the
touched bit, and the two copies couple with matrix element of absolute
value \(1/2\).
Both numbers lie in \((-8,8)\).
Inside that interval the resolvent is not the real function \eqref{eq:Gell},
and \(\mathcal{G}(E)=\pm 1\) has no solution there.
The projection onto the flat band is not the spectrum of \(H\).

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:stubs}
Every eigenstate of \(H_0\) that vanishes at both endpoints of the stitch
remains an eigenstate of \(H_0+V\) with the same energy.
On the \(L\)-torus the joint evaluation \(\psi\mapsto(\psi(a),\psi(b))\) is
surjective on \(\ker H_0\), so the nullity drops by exactly \(2\).
The two lost dimensions reappear as the two eigenvalues of the torus
outside \([-8,8]\).
In the infinite-sheet limit those eigenvalues are the pair \(\pm E_*\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:stitch}.
An odd state on a copy remains a zero mode precisely when its cell
function vanishes at the cell of that copy's endpoint.
The bit of the endpoint does not impose a second condition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(H_0\psi=E\psi\) and \(\psi(a)=\psi(b)=0\), then \(V\psi=0\).
The odd state supported on the first copy, with cell function a delta at
the cell of \(a\), is a zero mode by Lemma~\ref{lem:sectors} and takes a
nonzero value at \(a\) and the value \(0\) at \(b\).
The symmetric construction on the second copy separates the two
coordinates, so the evaluation has rank \(2\).
On the \(L\)-torus, where the kernel is finite,
\[
\dim\bigl(\ker H_0\cap\{\psi(a)=\psi(b)=0\}\bigr)=\dim\ker H_0-2.
\]
The perturbed kernel is no larger.
If \((H_0+V)\psi=0\), then \(H_0\psi=\psi(b)|a\rangle+\psi(a)|b\rangle\).
Every \(\varphi\in\ker H_0\) is orthogonal to \(H_0\psi\), so
\(\psi(b)\,\varphi(a)^*+\psi(a)\,\varphi(b)^*=0\).
The two test states force \(\psi(a)=\psi(b)=0\), and therefore \(H_0\psi=0\).
On an odd state the two bits of a cell carry opposite nonzero multiples of
the same cell amplitude, so vanishing at either bit is the condition that
the cell amplitude vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:cellpole}
Outside the spectrum of one copy, the resolvent between vertices
\((x,b)\) and \((y,b')\) is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cellpole}
\langle x,b|(E-H)^{-1}|y,b'\rangle
=\frac{\delta_{xy}}{2E}\,s(b,b')
+\frac12\bigl[(E+2A_\square)^{-1}\bigr]_{xy},
\end{equation}
where \(s(b,b')=+1\) when the bits agree and \(s(b,b')=-1\) when they differ.
The first term is the flat band. It is supported on a single cell.
The second term is the resolvent of \(-2A_\square\) and does not see the bit.
On \(J_3\) the same formula holds with \(A_{\mathrm{cubic}}\) in place of
\(A_\square\), for \(E\notin[-12,12]\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sectors} splits the Hilbert space into odd and even states,
and \(H\) preserves both. On the odd summand \(H=0\), so the resolvent is
\(1/E\). The odd projector acts cell by cell: its matrix on the two bits is
\(\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\), and it has no entry
between distinct cells. That is the first term.
On the even summand the map \(\varphi\mapsto\psi\), with both bits equal to
\(\varphi/\sqrt{2}\), intertwines \(H\) with \(-2A_\square\).
A site vector's even part is independent of the bit and equals that map
applied to \(e_x/\sqrt{2}\). The resulting matrix element is half the
square-lattice resolvent, for every pair of bits.
The \(J_3\) case is the same splitting from Lemma~\ref{lem:j3sectors}.
\end{proof}

The flat pole does not couple distinct cells. In the compressed resolvent
of several stubs, a \(1/E\) entry appears only when two stubs occupy the
same cell, and then only with the sign \(s(b,b')\). Stubs in different
cells communicate through \(-2A_\square\) alone.

For a finite set of stitches, with adjacency \(A_W\) supported on the stub
set \(\Sigma\), the same resolvent identity says that
\(E\notin[-8,8]\) is an eigenvalue if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:detstitch}
\det\bigl(I+\mathcal{G}_\Sigma(E)\,A_W\bigr)=0,
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{G}_\Sigma(E)\) is the resolvent of \(H_0\) compressed to \(\Sigma\).
It is block diagonal across copies.
If the stitches form a matching and each copy carries at most one stub,
then \(\mathcal{G}_\Sigma\) is a scalar matrix \(\mathcal{G}(E)I\) and \(A_W\) is a permutation of
disjoint transpositions, so \eqref{eq:detstitch} reduces to \(\mathcal{G}(E)^2=1\)
with multiplicity equal to the number of stitches.
Odd zero modes survive on each copy subject to one linear condition per
distinct stub cell of that copy.
Cells touched twice, or on both bits, still impose one condition.
A sheet with no stub keeps its whole odd kernel.
The count of surviving flat zeros is fixed by the set of stub cells, not
by the number of stitches and not by \(\ell_W\).

\section{The same stitch on \texorpdfstring{\(J_3\)}{J3}}
\label{sec:stitch3}

The twelve generators of \(J_3\) are the axis steps \(\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\),
each once with the sheet bit fixed and once with the sheet bit flipped.
The sheet exchange transposes two axes.
That transposition preserves the set of six axis steps, so the cells
adjacent to \((x,y,z)\) are the same six cells on each bit.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:j3sectors}
If \(\psi(x,y,z,1)=-\psi(x,y,z,0)\) at every cell, then \(A\psi=0\).
If the two bits carry the same cell function
\(\varphi\), then
\[
(A\psi)(x,y,z,b)=2(A_{\mathrm{cubic}}\varphi)(x,y,z)
\]
at both values of the bit.
The eigenvalues of \(H=-A\) on the even Bloch waves are
\(-4(\cos q_x+\cos q_y+\cos q_z)\), with range \([-12,12]\), and every odd
state is a zero mode.
On the \(L\)-torus the odd space has dimension \(L^3\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The twelve neighbours of a cell are its six adjacent cells on each bit.
An odd state cancels them in pairs.
An even state counts each adjacent cell twice, which is twice the cubic
adjacency.
The symbol of that adjacency is \(2(\cos q_x+\cos q_y+\cos q_z)\), and the
factor \(-2\) from \(H=-A\) produces the displayed band.
The sum of three cosines fills \([-3,3]\), so the band fills \([-12,12]\).
\end{proof}

The diagonal resolvent of one copy is again half the flat pole plus half
the dispersive average. Write \(\varepsilon_3(q)=-4(\cos q_x+\cos q_y+\cos q_z)\)
and
\[
\mathcal{G}_3(E)
=\frac{1}{2E}
+\frac12\int_{[0,2\pi]^3}\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\,
\frac{1}{E-\varepsilon_3(q)}
\]
for \(E\notin[-12,12]\).
The shift \(q\mapsto q+(\pi,\pi,\pi)\) sends \(\varepsilon_3\) to \(-\varepsilon_3\),
so \(\mathcal{G}_3\) is odd.
On \((12,\infty)\) it is strictly decreasing.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:j3stitch}
The limit \(\mathcal{G}_3(12+)\) exists and is finite, and
\[
\mathcal{G}_3(12+)<\frac{11}{48}<1.
\]
Two infinite copies of \(J_3\) joined by one stitch of hopping \(1\)
therefore have no eigenvalue outside \([-12,12]\).
The secular equation is \(\mathcal{G}_3(E)^2=1\), and it has no root in
that region.
The nullity statement of Proposition~\ref{prop:stubs} applies with cells
in \(\mathbb{Z}^3\): the torus nullity drops by exactly \(2\), and an odd state
survives when its cell function vanishes at the stub cell.
Those two dimensions do not reappear as eigenvalues outside the band.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(E>12\) the integrand is positive and increases as \(E\downarrow 12\),
so the limit exists in \((0,\infty]\).
It remains to show that the limit is finite and smaller than \(11/48\).
At the edge,
\[
12-\varepsilon_3(q)=4\bigl(3+\cos q_x+\cos q_y+\cos q_z\bigr),
\]
so the dispersive half of the limit equals \(I/8\), where
\[
I=\int_{[0,2\pi]^3}\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\,
\frac{1}{3+\cos q_x+\cos q_y+\cos q_z},
\]
and the flat pole contributes \(1/24\).
For \(a>0\), \(1/a=\int_0^\infty e^{-at}\,dt\).
The denominator is positive almost everywhere.
Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
I=\int_0^\infty e^{-3t}\,I_0(t)^3\,dt,
\qquad
I_0(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{t\cos\theta}\,d\theta.
\]
Indeed \(e^{-t\cos q}=e^{t\cos(q+\pi)}\), so each axis contributes a factor
\(I_0(t)\).

The bound \(I_0(t)\le e^{t}\) is immediate from \(\cos\theta\le 1\).
Thus \(e^{-3t}I_0(t)^3\le 1\) and
\[
\int_0^1 e^{-3t}I_0(t)^3\,dt\le 1.
\]
For the tail, use \(\cos\theta-1=-2\sin^2(\theta/2)\) and the symmetry that
halves the period:
\[
I_0(t)=\frac{e^{t}}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}e^{-2t\sin^2(\theta/2)}\,d\theta
=\frac{2e^{t}}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}e^{-2t\sin^2\phi}\,d\phi.
\]
On \([0,\pi/2]\) the concavity of sine gives \(\sin\phi\ge 2\phi/\pi\), so
\[
\int_0^{\pi/2}e^{-2t\sin^2\phi}\,d\phi
<\int_0^{\infty}e^{-(8t/\pi^2)\phi^2}\,d\phi
=\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8t}}.
\]
Multiplying by the prefactor \(2e^{t}/\pi\) gives
\(I_0(t)<e^{t}\sqrt{\pi/(8t)}\) for \(t>0\), and
\[
\int_1^{\infty}e^{-3t}I_0(t)^3\,dt
<\int_1^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{8t}\Bigr)^{3/2}\,dt
=2\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{8}\Bigr)^{3/2}.
\]
The comparison \(2(\pi/8)^{3/2}<1/2\) is \(\pi^3<32\).
Since \(\pi<22/7\) and
\[
\Bigl(\frac{22}{7}\Bigr)^3=\frac{10648}{343}<32=\frac{10976}{343},
\]
the tail is strictly less than \(1/2\).
Therefore \(I<3/2\), and
\[
\mathcal{G}_3(12+)
=\frac{1}{24}+\frac{I}{8}
<\frac{1}{24}+\frac{3}{16}
=\frac{11}{48}.
\]
For \(E>12\) one has \(\mathcal{G}_3(E)<\mathcal{G}_3(12+)\), so
\(\mathcal{G}_3(E)=1\) has no root in \([12,\infty)\).
Oddness rules out \(\mathcal{G}_3(E)=-1\) in \((-\infty,-12]\).
The resolvent argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:stitch} then produces no
eigenvalue outside \([-12,12]\).
The kernel count is the pairing argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:stubs},
with the odd delta on a cell of \(\mathbb{Z}^3\) in place of a cell of \(\mathbb{Z}^2\).
\end{proof}

The projection of the stitch onto the odd sector is the same rank-one
coupling of strength \(1/2\) as on \(J_2\).
The numbers \(\pm 1/2\) lie in \((-12,12)\) and are not eigenvalues.
On \(J_2\) the resolvent diverges at the edge, so a root of
\(\mathcal{G}(E)=1\) is forced to exist.
On \(J_3\) the same edge is a quadratic maximum in three momenta, the
integral of \(1/|q|^2\) converges, and the resolvent stays below \(11/48\).
For a stitch of hopping \(t\) the secular equation is
\(\mathcal{G}_3(E)=\pm 1/t\).
A root with \(|E|>12\) would require \(1/|t|<\mathcal{G}_3(12+)\).
Every \(|t|\le 48/11\) fails that inequality, and the sheet hopping \(t=1\)
is in this range.
On \(J_2\) the edge divergence means every \(t\neq 0\) produces a pair
outside \([-8,8]\).

On the \(L\)-torus with \(L\) even, the momentum \((\pi,\pi,\pi)\) lies on
the grid, so the finite resolvent has a simple pole at \(12\) and at \(-12\).
Each pole sends the secular function to infinity, and the function tends
to \(0\) at infinity, so each even torus does have one eigenvalue above
\(12\) and one below \(-12\).
Any limit of these eigenvalues along even \(L\) lies in \([-12,12]\):
a limit \(E_\infty>12\) would satisfy \(\mathcal{G}_3(E_\infty)=1\).

\section{The edge joining the two bits of one cell}
\label{sec:rung}

Every generator of \(J_2\) or of \(J_3\) that flips the sheet bit also changes
the cell.
The two bits of one cell are not adjacent.
An edge of hopping \(t\) between those two bits is a different modification
from a stitch between two copies.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:rung}
On one copy of \(J_2\), or of \(J_3\), add an edge of hopping \(t\neq 0\)
between the two bits of a single cell \(c\).
Let \(\psi\) be the odd delta of that cell, with amplitudes
\(1/\sqrt{2}\) and \(-1/\sqrt{2}\) on the two bits and with every other
amplitude zero.
Then \(H\psi=t\psi\).
Every odd state whose cell function vanishes at \(c\) remains a zero mode.
On the even summand the same edge is the on-site potential \(-t\) at \(c\).
Write \(g(E)\) for the on-site entry of \((E+2A)^{-1}\), with
\(A=A_\square\) on \(J_2\) and \(A=A_{\mathrm{cubic}}\) on \(J_3\).
The eigenvalues outside the even band are the energy \(t\), when \(|t|\)
exceeds that band, together with the solutions of \(g(E)=-1/t\).
On \(J_2\), for \(E>8\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rungG}
g(E)=\frac{2}{\pi E}\,K\Bigl(\frac{8}{E}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(K\) is the elliptic integral of \eqref{eq:Gell}.
The function \(g\) is odd, and on \((8,\infty)\) it falls strictly from
\(+\infty\) to \(0\).
Every \(t\neq 0\) therefore produces exactly one even eigenvalue outside
\([-8,8]\), below the band when \(t>0\) and above the band when \(t<0\).
On \(J_3\) the edge value is \(g(12+)=I/4\), with \(I\) the integral in the
proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:j3stitch}, so \(g(12+)<3/8\).
Every \(|t|\le 8/3\) produces no even eigenvalue outside \([-12,12]\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pure adjacency annihilates \(\psi\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:sectors} or
Lemma~\ref{lem:j3sectors}.
The added edge sends one bit to the other.
On \(\psi\) the two bits are opposite, so that exchange contributes \(-\psi\),
and \(H=-A\) turns the minus into the eigenvalue \(+t\).
An odd state that vanishes at \(c\) vanishes on both bits, so the added edge
contributes nothing.

The unitary map in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cellpole} intertwines
the pure Hamiltonian with \(-2A\).
Both bits of an even state carry the same cell function, and the added edge
multiplies the value of that function at \(c\) by \(-t\).
The even operator is therefore \(-2A-t|c\rangle\langle c|\).
For \(E\) outside the spectrum of \(-2A\),
\[
(E+2A+t|c\rangle\langle c|)\varphi=0
\]
if and only if \(\varphi=-t\,\varphi(c)\,(E+2A)^{-1}|c\rangle\).
Evaluating at \(c\) yields \(g(E)=-1/t\), unless \(\varphi=0\).
The odd eigenvalue \(t\) lies outside the even band precisely when \(|t|\)
exceeds the edge.

On \(J_2\), Proposition~\ref{prop:cellpole} and \eqref{eq:Gell} give
\(\mathcal{G}(E)=1/(2E)+g(E)/2\), which is the displayed formula.
For \(E>8\) every Bloch denominator \(E-\varepsilon(q)\) is positive and
strictly decreasing, so \(g\) falls strictly from \(+\infty\) to \(0\).
The shift \(q\mapsto q+(\pi,\pi)\) sends \(\varepsilon\) to \(-\varepsilon\), so
\(g\) is odd.
The value \(-1/t\) lies in \((0,\infty)\) exactly when \(t<0\), and then at
one energy above \(8\).
Oddness places the root below \(-8\) when \(t>0\).

On \(J_3\) the same half-and-half splitting gives
\(g(12+)=2\bigl(\mathcal{G}_3(12+)-1/24\bigr)=I/4\).
The bound \(I<3/2\) yields \(g(12+)<3/8\).
On \((12,\infty)\) the entry \(g\) falls strictly through \((0,g(12+))\).
A root of \(g(E)=-1/t\) outside \([-12,12]\) therefore requires
\(1/|t|<g(12+)\), and every \(|t|\le 8/3\) fails that inequality.
\end{proof}

The eigenvalue \(t\) is supported on two vertices.
It remains an eigenstate when \(|t|\) lies inside the even band: the added
edge preserves the odd summand, and the odd spectrum of the pure sheet is
\(\{0\}\).

A stitch of hopping \(t\) between two copies removes two kernel dimensions.
On \(J_2\) it binds a pair next to both edges, and on \(J_3\) it binds nothing
outside the band when \(|t|\le 48/11\).
The edge inside one cell moves one flat mode from \(0\) to \(t\), and it
binds at most one even eigenvalue, on the side opposite the sign of \(t\).

On the \(L\)-torus the odd multiplicity of \(0\) drops by exactly \(1\).
If some even zero mode is nonzero at \(c\), the even multiplicity of \(0\)
also drops by exactly \(1\), for every \(t\neq 0\).
A vector with \(-2A\varphi=t\,\varphi(c)\,|c\rangle\) pairs to zero against
that mode, which forces \(\varphi(c)=0\) and then forces \(\varphi\) into the
even kernel.
The momentum \(0\) lies on every grid, so the even bottom equals the
infinite-volume edge, and every \(t>0\) produces one even eigenvalue below
that edge.
The opposite edge lies on the grid only for even \(L\).
When it is absent, a negative hopping can leave the finite band and still
remain inside \((-8,8)\) on \(J_2\), or inside \((-12,12)\) on \(J_3\).
When the edge momentum is present, the finite resolvent has a pole there,
so an even eigenvalue sits outside the finite band.
On \(J_2\) any limit of these eigenvalues along growing tori satisfies
\(g(E)=-1/t\) and therefore stays outside \([-8,8]\).
On \(J_3\), if \(1/|t|\ge g(12+)\), every such limit lies back in
\([-12,12]\).

\section{Two stitches into the two bits of one cell}
\label{sec:twobit}

The flat pole of Proposition~\ref{prop:cellpole} distinguishes the two bits
of one cell from two stubs in different cells.
Take three copies, and join them by two edges of the same hopping \(t\neq 0\):
one edge from a vertex \(a\) on the first copy to bit \(0\) of a cell \(c\) on
the third, and one edge from a vertex \(b\) on the second copy to bit \(1\)
of the same cell.
Each outer copy carries one stub.
The host carries both bits of one cell.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:twobit}
Let \(\mathcal{G}\) be the diagonal resolvent entry of one pure copy, and
let \(g\) be the on-site entry of Proposition~\ref{prop:rung}.
Outside the even band, \(E\) is an eigenvalue if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:twobit}
t^2\,\mathcal{G}(E)\,g(E)=1
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
t^2\,\frac{\mathcal{G}(E)}{E}=1.
\end{equation}
On \(J_2\) each equation has exactly one root above \(8\) and, by oddness of
\(\mathcal{G}\) and of \(g\), the partner of that root below \(-8\).
On \(J_3\) one has \(g(12+)<3/8\) and \(\mathcal{G}_3(12+)<11/48\), so
\[
\mathcal{G}_3(12+)\,g(12+)<\frac{11}{128},
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal{G}_3(12+)}{12}<\frac{11}{576}.
\]
Every \(|t|\le\sqrt{128/11}\) fails the first equation outside \([-12,12]\),
and every \(|t|\le 24/\sqrt{11}\) fails the second.
In particular the sheet hopping \(t=1\) produces no eigenvalue outside
\([-12,12]\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The compression \eqref{eq:detstitch} applies to these four stubs, with the
band of the host in place of \([-8,8]\) on \(J_3\): an energy outside that
band is an eigenvalue precisely when the secular determinant on the stubs
vanishes.
Write \(|e\rangle\) and \(|o\rangle\) for the normalized even and odd states of
the cell \(c\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:cellpole} puts the resolvent entry of \(|e\rangle\)
equal to \(g(E)\) and the resolvent entry of \(|o\rangle\) equal to \(1/E\).
The two outer vertices lie on different copies, so the resolvent is diagonal
across them, with entry \(\mathcal{G}(E)\) at each.
The combinations \(|p\rangle=(|a\rangle+|b\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\) and

resolvent entry \(\mathcal{G}(E)\).
The added edges contribute
\(-t\) times the exchange of \(a\) with bit \(0\) and of \(b\) with bit \(1\).
The only nonzero matrix elements of that perturbation among these four
states are
\[
\langle p|V|e\rangle=-t,
\qquad
\langle m|V|o\rangle=-t.
\]
The even pair and the odd pair decouple.
On a pair with resolvent entries \(\mathcal{G}\) and \(\gamma\) and with
off-diagonal \(-t\), the secular equation is \(t^2\mathcal{G}\gamma=1\).
The even pair has \(\gamma=g(E)\), and the odd pair has \(\gamma=1/E\).

On \(J_2\), for \(E>8\), both \(\mathcal{G}\) and \(g\) fall strictly from

Both \(\mathcal{G}\) and \(g\) are odd, hence \(\mathcal{G}g\) and

On \(J_3\) the same two functions remain positive and strictly decreasing on
\((12,\infty)\), from a finite edge value.

\end{proof}

The first factor is the even channel of the cell.

\section{Codimension along a unitary orbit}
\label{sec:codim}

Between events, \(i\partial_t\psi=H(G)\psi\) with \(G\) and \(Q\) fixed,
and \(\partial H/\partial Q=0\).
VACEXC0 gives the same carrier evolution on every JOINT background.
Arrival times read from \(G\), or from the linear packet, are constant on
each inter-event interval.
Any scale factor built from those times has \(\dot a=0\) there.
A frozen texture in the flat band does not move \(H\).
Orientations that are functions of \(G\) are likewise constant.
A deterministic drift of \(M_{ab}\) would be an update rule for \(G\),
which the present equations do not contain.
REWIRE0 leaves degree-preserving re-pairings tied, so there is also no
bond drift that would rotate a frame.

Unweighted edges have combinatorial length \(1\).
A non-integer dilation is not an automorphism of a fixed simple graph.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:mu}
Across a sum-map split let \(\mu\) be the difference between the split-side
sum \(p+q\) and the merged-side amplitude at the same vertex.
Then \(\mu(t_0)=0\) for every fibre point, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mudot}
\dot\mu(t_0)=i\Bigl(s+\sum_{v\in C}\psi_v\Bigr),
\end{equation}
which does not depend on \(d=\psi_i-\psi_j\).
The \(d\)-dependent piece of \(\ddot\mu(t_0)\) is
\(-\tfrac12 d\bigl(|X_i|-|X_j|\bigr)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From \(i\partial_t\psi=-A\psi\) one has \(\partial_t\psi=iA\psi\).
On the split graph,
\[
\partial_t(p+q)=i\Bigl(s+\sum_{X_i}\psi+\sum_{X_j}\psi+2\sum_C\psi\Bigr).
\]
On the merged graph the exclusive and common neighbours each appear once.
Subtracting cancels the exclusive sums and leaves \eqref{eq:mudot}.
Differentiating the exclusive sums brings down \(|X_i|p\) and \(|X_j|q\),
and \(p-q=d\) produces the second-derivative coefficient.
\end{proof}

If \(s+\sum_C\psi\neq 0\), no choice of \(d\) restores \(C^1\) continuity of
the summed amplitude.
A uniform nonzero state fails \eqref{eq:mudot} on every cover.
A STORE replay meets every order because the recovered state is the
predecessor, not because \(d\) was tuned.
An asymmetric cover that has already passed \eqref{eq:mudot} fixes at most
one \(d\) at the next order. Higher orders then overdetermine it.

Equation~\eqref{eq:mudot} is two real constraints.
A real condition \(B_{ij}(t)=B_*\) is one.
Along the unitary orbit, \(B_{ij}(t)\) is almost periodic, with frequencies
equal to eigenvalue differences of \(H\).
For a two-mode parent
\(B(t)=B_0+C\cos(\omega t+\varphi)\),
crossings of a level inside the range exist and sit at an arccosine comb.
A one-dimensional orbit misses a codimension-\(2\) submanifold for generic
initial data, and meets a codimension-\(1\) level set in isolated roots.
The ratios inside \(B_*\) are unfixed (the conditional conservation identity
leaves two ratios free).
The family of roots, or of Rice rates
\[
\lambda(B_*)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{m_2}{m_0}}
\exp\Bigl(-\frac{(B_*-\mu)^2}{2m_0}\Bigr)
\]
for a stationary Gaussian approximation, is determined by the spectral
measure of \(B_{ij}(t)\) once the level is given.
A two-mode parent is the comb, not a constant hazard.
The exact codimension-\(2\) condition has rate zero.

Radiative decay of an in-band resonance is a third clock, with width
controlled by \eqref{eq:dos}, and it does not read \(Q\).
You could separate a parent with an empty store and an eigenvalue inside
the band from a parent that is spectrally bound and carries a stored
\(\xi\).
The first clock is the unitary width.
The second, if it splits, replays \(\xi\) and the daughters are the STORE
preimage.
The marginal of \(P_-\) on a Haar pair is Theorem~\ref{thm:haar},
with no free parameter.
A departure from that law, from the arcsine law, and from a point mass at
\(1/2\) would be a different distribution.
Agreement with one of the three is the corresponding ensemble.

\section{The fibre circle}
\label{sec:circle}

The sum map fixes \(s\) and leaves \(d\in\C\).
The bond and the pair current are two real coordinates on that line,
not two different mechanisms.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:BJ}
Let \(\mathcal{J}_{ij}=\Im(\psi_i^*\psi_j)\), so that
\(\psi_i^*\psi_j=B_{ij}+i\mathcal{J}_{ij}\).
Then
\begin{align*}
B_{ij}&=\frac{|s|^2-|d|^2}{4},&
\mathcal{J}_{ij}&=\frac12\Im(\bar d\, s),\\
B_{ij}^2+\mathcal{J}_{ij}^2&=|\psi_i|^2|\psi_j|^2.
\end{align*}
At hopping \(1\), the continuity flux on the bond is \(2\mathcal{J}_{ij}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \(\psi_i=(s+d)/2\) and \(\psi_j=(s-d)/2\).
The cross term \(d^*s-s^*d\) is purely imaginary and equals
\(2i\Im(d^*s)\), which produces the formula for \(\mathcal{J}_{ij}\).
The identity \(B^2+\mathcal{J}^2=|\psi_i|^2|\psi_j|^2\) is
\(|\psi_i^*\psi_j|^2=|\psi_i|^2|\psi_j|^2\).
The factor \(2\) in the flux is the divergence convention
\(\sum_{j\sim i}2\Im(\psi_i^*\psi_j)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:circle}
Fix \(s\) and a real level \(B_*\).
The fibre set \(B(s,d)=B_*\) is empty when \(B_*>|s|^2/4\),
a single point when equality holds, and the circle
\[
|d|^2=|s|^2-4B_*
\]
when \(B_*<|s|^2/4\).
The weight \(P_-\), and therefore \(h_2(P_-)\), is constant on that circle.
The endpoint identification \(d\mapsto -d\) preserves the circle,
preserves \(B\), and sends \(\mathcal{J}\) to \(-\mathcal{J}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:BJ} rearranges to the displayed radius.
\(P_-\) depends on the pair only through \(|d|^2/(|s|^2+|d|^2)\).
Replacing \(d\) by \(-d\) changes the sign of \(\Im(\bar d\, s)\) and
does not change \(|d|\).
\end{proof}

A bond threshold is one real condition.
It selects the radius and leaves the angle.
The angle is the pair current.
Remark~\ref{rem:h2} sees only the radius, so an entropy built from
\(h_2\) is constant on a set of fibre states that the current separates.
The conditional balance
\(-\alpha-\beta(1+c)+2\gamma B=0\)
likewise fixes a radius when \(\gamma\neq 0\):
\(B_*=\bigl(\alpha+\beta(1+c)\bigr)/(2\gamma)\),
with the two ratios \(\alpha/\gamma\) and \(\beta/\gamma\) unfixed.
Those ratios move the circle.
They do not select a point on it.
Two real witnesses of a level set are two points of this circle.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:balanced}
If the exclusive neighbourhoods have equal cardinality,
the coefficient of \(d\) in \(\ddot\mu(t_0)\) vanishes.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:mu} gives that coefficient as
\(-\tfrac12\bigl(|X_i|-|X_j|\bigr)\).
\end{proof}

A symmetric cover therefore leaves the circle intact at the first two
orders of the summed amplitude.
The current is still free at those orders.
An asymmetric cover can pin \(d\) at second order, and then only the
value that cancels the imbalance.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:circle-energy}
At hopping \(1\), the field energy is \(E_\psi=-2\sum_e B_e\), so no
current appears in it.
On the circle of Proposition~\ref{prop:circle} the contracted bond \(B\)
is constant, and the contraction energy is
\[
\Delta E_\psi
=2B-\Re(s^* S_{\mathrm{ex}})-\Re(d^* V),
\]
where \(S_{\mathrm{ex}}\) is the sum of the amplitudes on the exclusive
neighbourhoods \(X_i\) and \(X_j\), and
\[
V=\sum_{n\in X_j}\psi_n-\sum_{m\in X_i}\psi_m.
\]
As the angle runs, \(\Delta E_\psi\) sweeps an interval of half-width
\(|d|\,|V|\) about \(2B-\Re(s^* S_{\mathrm{ex}})\).
The same half-width is the oscillation of the mixed piece of the event
account, \(R_{\mathrm{mixed}}=2\Sigma_{\mathrm{cross}}\).
The norm change \(2B\) and the fibre piece \(-2B\) stay constant.
\(V=0\) is the vanishing of that oscillation.
Equal cardinalities \(|X_i|=|X_j|\) do not imply it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sum \(E_\psi=-2\sum_e B_e\) is the quadratic form of \(H=-A\).
The ledger cross sum is
\(\Sigma_{\mathrm{cross}}
=\sum_{m\in X_i}\Re(\psi_j^*\psi_m)
+\sum_{n\in X_j}\Re(\psi_i^*\psi_n)\),
and \(\Delta E_\psi=2B-2\Sigma_{\mathrm{cross}}\).
Substitute \(\psi_i=(s+d)/2\) and \(\psi_j=(s-d)/2\).
The terms linear in \(d\) collect into \(\Re(d^* V)/2\), and the terms
without \(d\) collect into \(\Re(s^* S_{\mathrm{ex}})/2\).
Doubling produces the displayed formula.
On the circle \(B\), \(s\), and \(|d|\) are fixed, so the only variation
is \(-\Re(d^* V)\), whose range has half-width \(|d|\,|V|\).
The split of the event account into cover, fibre, and mixed pieces
identifies the mixed piece with \(2\Sigma_{\mathrm{cross}}\).
Common neighbours do not enter \(V\): their collapse cancels in
\(\Delta E_\psi\).
\end{proof}

\section{Additive bits on a matching}
\label{sec:bits}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:varh}
For \(h_2\) as in \eqref{eq:se},
\[
\int_0^1 h_2(p)^2\,dp
=\frac{\tfrac56-\pi^2/18}{(\ln 2)^2}.
\]
If \(P_-\) is uniform on \([0,1]\),
\[
\mathrm{Var}\bigl(h_2(P_-)\bigr)
=\frac{21-2\pi^2}{36(\ln 2)^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(A(p)=p\ln p+(1-p)\ln(1-p)\), so \(h_2=-A/\ln 2\) and the
integral of \(A^2\) is the numerator.
Symmetry gives
\[
\int_0^1 A^2
=2\int_0^1 p^2(\ln p)^2\,dp
+2\int_0^1 p(1-p)\ln p\ln(1-p)\,dp.
\]
The substitution \(p=e^{-u}\) yields
\(\int_0^1 p^2(\ln p)^2\,dp=2/27\).
The cross term is \(\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta B(\alpha,\beta)\) at
\((\alpha,\beta)=(2,2)\).
There \(B(2,2)=1/6\), \(\psi(2)-\psi(4)=-5/6\), and
\(\psi'(4)=\pi^2/6-49/36\), so
\[
\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta B(2,2)
=\frac1{6}\Bigl(\frac{37}{18}-\frac{\pi^2}{6}\Bigr)
=\frac{37}{108}-\frac{\pi^2}{36}.
\]
Adding the two contributions produces
\(\int A^2=5/6-\pi^2/18\).
The variance subtracts
\(\bigl(1/(2\ln 2)\bigr)^2\), and
\[
\frac56-\frac14-\frac{\pi^2}{18}
=\frac{7}{12}-\frac{\pi^2}{18}
=\frac{21-2\pi^2}{36}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:disjoint}
Let \(\psi\) be Haar-uniform on the unit sphere in \(\C^N\), and let
\(e_1,\ldots,e_k\) be pairwise disjoint coordinate pairs, \(2k\le N\).
Then the variables \(P_-(e_a)\) are i.i.d.\ uniform on \([0,1]\).
With \(s_e=h_2(P_-(e))\),
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}\sum_{a=1}^k s_{e_a}
&=\frac{k}{2\ln 2},\\
\mathrm{Var}\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^k s_{e_a}\Bigr)
&=k\,\frac{21-2\pi^2}{36(\ln 2)^2}.
\end{align*}
The mean and the variance are fixed by the same law.
No further parameter enters either.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Haar measure is the direction of a vector with i.i.d.\ standard complex
normal coordinates.
\(P_-\) on a pair depends only on those two coordinates and is invariant
under a global rescaling, so it is a function of the corresponding
\(\C^2\) marginal.
Disjoint pairs use disjoint coordinates and are independent.
Uniformity of each marginal is Theorem~\ref{thm:haar}.
The variance of one summand is Lemma~\ref{lem:varh}, and independence
adds the variances.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:cov}
If two pairs share a vertex, each marginal of \(P_-\) is still uniform,
so the expectations of \(h_2\) still add.
The covariance equals the variance of the conditional expectation of
\(h_2\) given the shared amplitude, and that variance is strictly positive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(f_{12}=h_2(P_-)\) on coordinates \((z_1,z_2)\), and \(f_{13}\) on
\((z_1,z_3)\), with \(z_a\) i.i.d.\ standard complex normal.
Conditioning on \(z_1\) makes \(z_2\) and \(z_3\) independent, so
\[
\mathbb{E}[f_{12}f_{13}]
=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\mathbb{E}[f_{12}\mid z_1]\bigr)^2\bigr].
\]
The covariance is \(\mathrm{Var}(\mathbb{E}[f_{12}\mid z_1])\).
For each fixed finite nonzero \(z_1\) the relative phase is uniform and
the amplitude ratio is finite, so \(h_2<1\) on a set of positive
probability and the conditional expectation is strictly less than \(1\).
As \(|z_1|\to\infty\) the ratio \(\lambda\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:lambda} tends to \(0\), and bounded convergence
gives conditional expectation tending to \(1\).
The law of \(|z_1|\) charges both regimes, so the conditional expectation
is not almost surely constant.
\end{proof}

A cut that is a matching of \(k\) legs therefore has bit sum
\(k/(2\ln 2)\) and variance proportional to \(k\), under the Haar law.
A cut whose legs share interior vertices keeps the same mean per leg and
replaces the variance by a sum of the covariances of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cov}.
The incidence graph of the cut is part of the fluctuation.
It is not part of the one-edge mean.
The dimension \(D_Q\) of Section~\ref{sec:kernel} does not enter this
variance: \(h_2\) is constant on the circle of Proposition~\ref{prop:circle},
while \(D_Q\) counts that circle's real plane.

A symmetric law of \(B/q\) has mean bond zero and, unless the law is a
point mass at \(0\), mean \(h_2\) strictly below \(1\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:taylor} makes the deficit even in \(B/q\).
Vanishing of \(\mathbb{E}[B]\) is a different condition from
\(h_2=1\).

\section{The current conditional on the bond}
\label{sec:condJ}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:condJ}
Let the pair be two i.i.d.\ standard complex normals, or equivalently
any fixed coordinate pair of a Haar vector.
Set \(x=B/q\) and \(y=\mathcal{J}/q\).
The joint law is supported on the disk \(x^2+y^2<1/4\), with density
\[
f(x,y)=\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{1-4(x^2+y^2)}}.
\]
The marginal of \(x\) is uniform on \([-1/2,1/2]\).
Conditional on \(x\),
\[
y=\sqrt{\tfrac14-x^2}\,\sin\Theta,
\]
where \(\Theta\) is uniform on \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\), and
\[
\mathrm{Var}(y\mid x)=\frac{1-4x^2}{8}.
\]
The differential entropy of this conditional law is
\[
\ln\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac14-x^2}\Bigr)
\]
nats.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For two i.i.d.\ exponential squared moduli the ratio
\(\rho=|\psi_i|^2/q\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and independent of the
relative phase \(\varphi\), which is itself uniform on \([0,2\pi)\).
Then \(x=\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}\cos\varphi\) and
\(y=\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}\sin\varphi\).
The push-forward of \(\rho(1-\rho)\) and of the uniform angle, written in
polar coordinates on the \((x,y)\) plane and divided by the radius,
is the displayed density.
Its integral in \(y\) equals \(1\) for each \(x\in(-1/2,1/2)\), which is
the marginal already implied by Theorem~\ref{thm:haar}.
The change of variables \(y=\sqrt{1/4-x^2}\,\sin\Theta\) flattens the
conditional density to \(d\Theta/\pi\) on \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\).
The second moment of \(\sin\Theta\) on that interval is \(1/2\), so the
conditional variance is \((1/4-x^2)/2=(1-4x^2)/8\).
The same change of variables, together with
\(\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\cos\theta)\,d\theta=-(\pi/2)\ln 2\),
produces the differential entropy.
\end{proof}

The density depends on the pair only through \(x^2+y^2\).
The ensemble is isotropic in the \((B,\mathcal{J})\) plane.
The optional bit \(h_2\) is not: it is a function of \(x\) alone.
Conditioning on \(h_2\) fixes \(|x|\) and leaves the conditional law above.
That differential entropy is not a summand of \(h_2\).
One is the Shannon entropy of a binary weight.
The other is a continuous entropy of a current.
Section~\ref{sec:kernel} counts the real plane in which both sit, and
counts it once.

If the two moduli are equal and the phase is uniform, then
\(x^2+y^2=1/4\), so \(y=\pm\sqrt{1/4-x^2}\).
The conditional law collapses to a sign.
The ground state of Proposition~\ref{prop:ground} is the further collapse
\(x=\pm 1/2\), \(y=0\).
The amount of current left after the bond is fixed is a property of the
ensemble.

\section{Separable symbols, to every order}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:sep}
Suppose \(\varepsilon(q)=\sum_{i=1}^d f_i(q_i)\).
Every partial derivative that involves two distinct momenta vanishes
identically, so every mixed monomial has coefficient zero.
Both dispersive bands are of this form,
\[
\varepsilon_{J_2}=-4J(\cos q_x+\cos q_y),\qquad
\varepsilon_{J_3}=-4J\sum_{i=1}^3\cos q_i.
\]
No order of either Taylor series satisfies \(\gamma/\beta=2\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A mixed partial of a sum of univariate functions is zero.
The degree-\(4\) rotational invariant and the degree-\(4\) term of
\(\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2|q|^2}\) both contain \(q_i^2 q_j^2\) with
\(\gamma/\beta=2\), as in Theorem~\ref{thm:ratio}.
A coefficient that is zero at every order cannot match a coefficient
that is not.
\end{proof}

The \(J_2\) generators are axis steps, half of them flipping the sheet bit.

It does not produce a mixed monomial in \((q_x,q_y)\).
A spatial generator along \(x+y\) would.

\(q_x^2 q_y^2\).
The obstruction of Theorem~\ref{thm:ratio} is the absence of those
generators, not a truncation.

\section{One length, three readings of its fluctuations}
\label{sec:moments}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:rayleigh}
Let stitch endpoints be a homogeneous Poisson process of intensity
\(\rho\) on the sheet, and set \(\ell_W=\rho^{-1/2}\).
The distance \(R\) from a sheet point to the nearest endpoint satisfies
\[
\mathbb{E}[R]=\frac12\ell_W,\qquad
\frac{\mathrm{Var}(R)}{(\mathbb{E}[R])^2}=\frac4\pi-1.
\]
The coefficient of variation does not depend on \(\rho\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The void probability is \(\mathbb{P}(R>r)=\exp(-\rho\pi r^2)\).
Hence
\[
\mathbb{E}[R]
=\int_0^\infty\exp(-\pi\rho r^2)\,dr
=\frac12\rho^{-1/2}.
\]
The random variable \(\pi R^2\) is exponential with mean \(1/\rho\), so
\(\mathbb{E}[R^2]=1/(\pi\rho)\).
Then
\[
\mathrm{Var}(R)
=\frac1{\pi\rho}-\frac1{4\rho}
=\frac{4-\pi}{4\pi\rho},
\]
and dividing by \((\mathbb{E}[R])^2=1/(4\rho)\) produces \(4/\pi-1\).
\end{proof}

The construction's length and the mean nearest-endpoint distance are the
same power of \(\lambda\), and they differ by the factor \(1/2\).
The relative width \(\sqrt{4/\pi-1}\) stays put as \(\lambda\to 0\).
Scale separation enlarges the mean axis ratio.
It does not tighten the transverse spacing about that mean.
In-plane shifts move the endpoints.
They do not change the law of \(R/\ell_W\).

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:avol}
Suppose the in-plane scale factor is \(1\) and the transverse factor is
\(a_\perp\).
The geometric mean of the three coordinate scales is \(a_\perp^{1/3}\).
If \(a_\perp\propto t^\alpha\), the geometric mean grows as \(t^{\alpha/3}\).
The isotropic substitution
\(\alpha\mapsto(2-3\alpha)/(3\alpha)\)
returns \((2-3\alpha)/(3\alpha)\) when \(\alpha\) is the transverse exponent
and \((2-\alpha)/\alpha\) when \(\alpha\) is replaced by the volume exponent
\(\alpha/3\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A coordinate cell has volume proportional to \(a_\perp\), so the geometric
mean of the three edge lengths is \(a_\perp^{1/3}\).
Insert \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha/3\) into \((2-3\alpha)/(3\alpha)\).
The second insertion is
\[
\frac{2-3\cdot(\alpha/3)}{3\cdot(\alpha/3)}=\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}.
\]
\end{proof}

No earned equation supplies \(\alpha\).
The two substitutions are what the same isotropic formula returns on the
two exponents that \eqref{eq:ds} distinguishes.
The difference \(H_\perp-H_\parallel\) is then of order the expansion rate
itself, because \(H_\parallel=0\) while \(a_\parallel\) is held at \(1\).
Nothing in \(H=-A\) damps that difference.
Section~\ref{sec:h-orient} is the same absence of a drift, read on
\(M_{ab}\) rather than on \(\ell_W\).

Balls of the graph distance are the balls of \eqref{eq:ds}.
The static symbol \(\tfrac12+2|q|^2\) decays in that graph distance, with
Helmholtz constant \(\kappa=1/2\).
In coordinates where a sheet step has size \(1\), the same ball has
transverse radius smaller by \(\ell_W\).
A short range in hopping units and a large axis ratio are the same ball
written in two coordinate systems.
The excluded gapless response would be constant on the same family of
ellipsoids, with \(\kappa=0\).

\section{Three readings of the same quadratic}
\label{sec:masses}

At \(J=1\) the dispersive edge of \(J_2\) is
\[
\varepsilon(q)=-8+2|q|^2-\frac16(q_x^4+q_y^4)+O(q^6).
\]
The quadratic piece is \(\tfrac12 q\cdot(4I)q\).
Matching it to \(|q|^2/(2m^*)\) fixes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mstar}
m^*=\frac14.
\end{equation}
The same match at general hopping is \(m^*=1/(4J)\), because both the
Hessian and the edge \(-8J\) scale with \(J\).
This \(m^*\) is the inverse curvature of the band.
It is a property of \(G\) through \(H=-A\), so it takes the same value on
every field, including every JOINT vacuum.

The constant \(-8\) is the eigenvalue of the normalised all-ones mode on
the \(8\)-regular graph.
An excitation energy measured from the edge does not contain it.
A binding energy measured below the edge is
\[
m_{\mathrm{gap}}=-8-E_0
\]
when \(E_0<-8\), and \(0\) otherwise.
Proposition~\ref{prop:clique} lower-bounds \(m_{\mathrm{gap}}\) by \(n-9\)
for an embedded clique of order \(n\ge 10\).
That bound is an eigenvalue difference in hopping units.
It is not \(m^*\), and it is not \(-8\).

A relativistic series
\(mc^2+|q|^2/(2m)-|q|^4/(8m^3c^2)+\cdots\)
asks the constant term, the curvature, and the quartic invariant to be
three readings of one mass.
Proposition~\ref{prop:sep} already forbids the quartic identification.
The constant term cannot take its place:
\(-8\) is shared by the sheet ground mode, so it cancels in every
difference from the edge, whether the difference is \(2|q|^2\) above the
edge or \(m_{\mathrm{gap}}\) below it.

Between events \(H\) is fixed, and on a translation-invariant sheet the
crystal momentum of a packet is conserved.
The in-plane energy \(2|q|^2\) therefore does not drift.
A growth of \(\ell_W\) that does not touch the edges inside a motif leaves
the Rayleigh quotient of that motif's trial vector unchanged, so
\(m_{\mathrm{gap}}\) stays fixed in hopping units while \(a_\perp\) grows
(Section~\ref{sec:h-fixedd}).
A mass that redshifts with \(a_\perp\) would be a further quotient of this
gap by the transverse scale.
The gap itself does not supply that quotient.

\section{Bond sum of an eigenstate}
\label{sec:starsum}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:starsum}
Let \(H=-A\) and \(H\psi=E\psi\). At every vertex
\[
\sum_{j\sim i}B_{ij}=-E\,|\psi_i|^2,
\qquad
\sum_{j\sim i}\mathcal{J}_{ij}=0.
\]
For a normalised eigenstate, \(\sum_e B_e=-E/2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalue equation is \(\sum_{j\sim i}\psi_j=-E\psi_i\).
Multiply by \(\psi_i^*\).
The right-hand side is real, so the imaginary part of the left-hand side
vanishes and the real part is the bond sum.
Summing the bond identity over vertices counts each edge twice and
gives \(2\sum_e B_e=-E\) after \(\sum_i|\psi_i|^2=1\).
The same factor is the quadratic form \(E_\psi=-2\sum_e B_e\).
\end{proof}

On \(8\)-regular \(J_2\) the normalised all-ones mode has \(E=-8\) and
\(\sum_e B_e=4\).
Any normalised eigenstate therefore satisfies
\[
\sum_e B_e-4=\frac{-E-8}{2}.
\]
A gap below the coordination floor is twice the excess of the total bond
over \(4\):
\[
m_{\mathrm{gap}}=-E-8=2\Bigl(\sum_e B_e-4\Bigr)
\]
whenever \(E<-8\).
Rearranging bonds without raising \(\sum_e B_e\) does not open that gap.
The flat band is the case \(E=0\): the bond sum at every vertex vanishes,
while a single \(B_{ij}\) may not.
A flat-band texture is not a quadrature state.

Every eigenstate, including a mode below the edge, has no net current at
any vertex.
A state inside the essential spectrum that is not an eigenstate of the
full \(H\) is not under this constraint.
Its density can leave the motif.
The width of that leakage is the golden-rule width controlled by
\eqref{eq:dos}.
The conditional law of Theorem~\ref{thm:condJ} describes one edge.
On an eigenstate the star of currents sums to zero, so that one edge does
not carry its current alone.

Between events the continuity equation is
\[
\partial_t|\psi_i|^2+\sum_{j\sim i}2\mathcal{J}_{ij}=0.
\]
A contraction replaces the unitary step.
The norm jumps by \(2B\), which is Lemma~\ref{lem:bond-expand} and is not
a divergence of \(\mathcal{J}\).
The waiting interval transports density by the current.
The event changes the norm by the bond.
\(Q\) enters neither identity, because it does not enter \(H\) and it is
not a term in \(B\).
