\section{A noncompact meeting direction}
\label{sec:h-macro}

A ring of finitely many sheets is three-dimensional only inside the window
of \eqref{eq:Vchain} and is a thick two-dimensional stack in the infrared.
A stable infrared exponent \(3\), built from locally two-dimensional
sheets, uses one of the following.

An infinite line, \(S=\infty\), with \(\ell_W\) finite.
Then \(V\sim r^3/\ell_W\) for every \(r\gg\ell_W\).
The third direction is noncompact, and the metric remains anisotropic by
\(\ell_W\) (Section~\ref{sec:h-iso}).

A meeting complex that is itself a noncompact three-dimensional foam, so
that no axis stays at a parametrically different cost.
That complex is not the chain construction, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:metric} does not apply to it.

A finite stack, however large, has compactification radius \(S\ell_W\).
The extrapolation of the chain past that radius is the infinite-line
formula, which still carries the axis ratio.
Section~\ref{sec:h-de} records what that ratio does to a scale factor.
