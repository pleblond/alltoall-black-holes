# Transport fixes the quadratic metric, not the quartic, and not a scale factor

The observer distance reconstructed from long-wave packets is the inverse
of the Hessian of \(\varepsilon(q)\) at the band bottom. On J2 and on J3
that Hessian is \(4J\,I\), so the transport metric is conformal to the
lattice metric at quadratic order, with a speed fixed by \(J\).

Changing stitch density changes the transverse entry of the Hessian
(`j3-weave-universality/note.md`) and therefore changes the observed
metric. That much of the ledger sentence is a theorem for the chain weave.
It does not imply that every change of substrate statistics changes the
metric: a pure flat-band texture does not enter \(H\) and does not move
arrival times (VACEXC0, VACTEXTURE).

The quartic part of \(\varepsilon\) is invisible to this metric and visible
to angular deficits (`lorentz-emergent/note.md`,
`manhattan-to-radial/note.md`). A transport fit that stops at the
quadratic action will report isotropy on J2 even though \(\gamma/\beta=0\).

A time-dependent transport metric requires a time-dependent \(H\), hence a
time-dependent \(G\). Between events the metric extracted this way is
constant (`dark-energy-weave/note.md`).
