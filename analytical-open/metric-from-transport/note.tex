\section{Transport metric from the Hessian}
\label{sec:h-metric}

The long-wave packet speed is the inverse of the Hessian of \(\varepsilon(q)\)
at the band bottom.
On \(J_2\) and on \(J_3\) that Hessian is \(4J\, I\), so the quadratic transport
metric is conformal to the lattice metric, with speed fixed by \(J\).
A stitch density changes the transverse entry
(Section~\ref{sec:h-j3w}) and therefore changes that metric.
A flat-band texture does not enter \(H\) and does not move arrival times.
The quartic part of \(\varepsilon\) is invisible to this metric and visible to
the angular deficit of Theorem~\ref{thm:ratio}.
A time-dependent transport metric requires a time-dependent \(H\), hence a
time-dependent graph.
Between events the metric extracted this way is constant
(Section~\ref{sec:codim}).
