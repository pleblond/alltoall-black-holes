# The crossover is \(\ell_W\), and it is also the anisotropy

The preregistration already fixes

\[
\ell_W(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}
\]

from the Poisson spacing of stitch endpoints. Short-time diffusion on the
homogenized chain sees only the sheet. The return probability of a random
walk with in-plane diffusion constant \(D_\parallel\) and transverse
constant \(D_\perp=\gamma\ell_W^2\) (sheet-index coordinate) is

\[
Z(t)=(4\pi D_\parallel t)^{-1}(4\pi D_\perp t)^{-1/2}
\]

in the window \(\ell_W^2/D_\parallel\ll t\ll (S\ell_W)^2/D_\perp\), so the
spectral dimension extracted from \(-2\,d\ln Z/d\ln t\) equals \(3\) exactly
inside that window, equals \(2\) at shorter time, and returns to \(2\) after
the ring is explored.

Graph distance, which is what \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)=d\ln V/d\ln r\) uses, is
not this walk. Its anisotropy is geometric: transverse coordinate distance
\(1\) costs graph length \(\ell_W\). The radius where \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\)
crosses \(2.5\) should track \(\ell_W(\lambda)\), which the preregistration
already gates. The additional prediction is that the same \(\ell_W\) is the
ratio of principal radii of the ball. One length, two plots. If the
crossing radius tracks \(\ell_W\) and the axis ratio does not, the ball is
not the homogenized chain and the volume exponent has another source.
