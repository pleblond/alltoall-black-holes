# The exponent on \(\rho_I\) is fixed by which intersection is counted

Hold a comoving population of surfaces inside an already expanding
isotropic volume, as a kinematic comparison only. Area density scales as
\(a^{-1}\). Length density of pairwise intersection lines scales as
\(a^{-2}\). Number density of triple points scales as \(a^{-3}\).

The ledger's alternative \(a\propto\rho_I^{-1/3}\) is the triple-point
count. It is not the law for line density, which would be
\(a\propto\rho_L^{-1/2}\), and it is not the law for sheet area. WEAVE's
\(\lambda\) is a stitch **number** per node, closer to a point process on
the sheet than to a triple point in the three-volume. The exponent \(-1/3\)
should not be used on \(\lambda\).

On the chain construction the derived ruler is
\(\ell_W\propto\lambda^{-1/2}\), so the transverse scale tracks
\(\lambda^{-1/2}\), an exponent \(-1/2\) on the stitch density that the
preregistration already computed. Replacing \(-1/2\) by \(-1/3\) changes
the predicted \(a_\perp(\lambda)\) by a power that a two-point ladder in
\(\lambda\) can see. Use the \(-1/2\) from the Poisson spacing unless a
different intersection is being counted, and say which.
