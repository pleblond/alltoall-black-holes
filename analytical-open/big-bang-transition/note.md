# The geometry-forming step is a drop in effective dimension, if it is a percolation of plaquettes

`graph-soup-geometry/note.md` identifies the geometric phase with a
plaquette percolation at fixed degree, not with an increase of
\(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\) out of \(3\). On the tree side of that transition the
ball grows exponentially, which a local logarithmic derivative
\(d\ln V/d\ln r\) reports as a large number. On the sheet side it reports
\(2\). Formation of geometry, in this order parameter, is a **decrease** of
the reported exponent toward \(2\), followed by a weave lift toward a
window at \(3\) if stitches percolate in a one-dimensional meeting
complex.

A numerical search that watches \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\) rise from \(0\) or
\(1\) toward \(3\) and calls the rise a big bang is aimed at the opposite
side of the transition. The signature to preregister is: \(f_\square\)
crossing a percolation threshold, \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\) falling from a
large tree/expander value onto a plateau at \(2\), and only afterwards a
possible window at \(3\) whose width is \(S\ell_W\).

No earned firing law produces \(f_\square(t)\). The test can still be done
on a static family parameterized by cycle density. That family is the phase
diagram. A dynamical \(t\) is a later parameterization of the same axis,
and only if some future rule changes \(f_\square\).
