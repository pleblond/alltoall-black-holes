# \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\) can vary with scale at fixed graph; that is not yet cosmology

On a pure power \(V=A(r/\ell)^d\) the logarithmic derivative is the
constant \(d\), independent of \(\ell\). On the chain weave it is a
function of the dimensionless ratio \(r/\ell_W\):

\[
d_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)\to
\begin{cases}
2 & r/\ell_W\to 0,\\
3 & 1\ll r/\ell_W\ll S,\\
2 & r/\ell_W\gg S.
\end{cases}
\]

An observer at a fixed graph distance sees a fixed exponent. An observer
who changes the scale they probe, on a fixed graph, walks along this
curve. That is a scale dependence, not a time dependence.

Time dependence of \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\) at fixed comoving probe requires
\(\ell_W=\ell_W(t)\) or \(S=S(t)\), hence a changing graph
(`dark-energy-weave/note.md`). Until then, "early universe" and "late
universe" are the small-\(r\) and large-\(r\) ends of one static curve.
Reporting them as two epochs double-counts the crossover.

The clean static plot is \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\) against \(\log(r/\ell_W)\) for
several \(\lambda\), collapsed onto one curve. Failure to collapse means
the single length \(\ell_W\) does not control the crossover, and the
preregistration's M1 formula is incomplete. Success means there is nothing
further to explain about "evolution" until \(G\) changes.
