# The causal cone is Manhattan; the energy peak is radial at long wavelength

On J2, group velocity of the dispersive band is
\(v=(4\sin q_x,\,4\sin q_y)\). Maxima, earned:

\[
|v|_{\mathrm{axial}}=4,\qquad
|v|_{\mathrm{Euclidean}}=4\sqrt{2},\qquad
|v_x|+|v_y|=8.
\]

On J3 the same pattern with \(d=3\): axial \(4\), Euclidean \(4\sqrt{3}\),
Manhattan \(12\). For the separable symbol
\(\varepsilon=-4\sum_{i=1}^d\cos q_i\) the general relation is

\[
\frac{v_E^{\max}}{v_M}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}},
\]

attained at \(q_i=\pi/2\).

The Lieb–Robinson cone of \(H=-A\) is the Manhattan ball of radius \(v_M t\),
because each generator hop is axis-aligned and the degree sets the bound
(EM-0I: Manhattan \(8\) on J2). A packet with momentum \(q\) near \(0\) has
\(v=4q+O(q^3)\), parallel to \(q\) up to the \(O(q^2)\) deflection in
`lorentz-emergent/note.md`. Superposing a localized source fills a
Euclidean ball at the peak. The diamond corners are the support of the
forerunner, exponentially small relative to the peak when the packet is
narrow in momentum.

**What to measure.** A fixed-fraction-of-peak shell should be round, with a
quadrupole \(O(\langle q^2\rangle)\). A threshold pushed into the
forerunner should turn up the Manhattan corners. Reading one of these as
the geometry and the other as a failure is a threshold choice. The ratio
\(v_E^{\max}/v_M=1/\sqrt{d}\) is the sharpest single number: \(1/\sqrt{2}\)
on J2, \(1/\sqrt{3}\) on J3. It does not require a continuum fit.
