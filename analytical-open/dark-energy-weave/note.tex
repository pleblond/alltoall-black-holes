\section{A scale factor that the carrier does not generate}
\label{sec:h-de}

Between structural events the carrier is
\(i\partial_t\psi=H(G)\psi\) with \(H=-A\) and \(Q\) frozen.
Graph distance, resistance distance, and linear arrival time are
properties of \(G\).
They are constant on each inter-event interval, so any scale factor read
from them has \(\dot a=0\) there (Section~\ref{sec:codim}).
A flat-band texture does not enter \(H\) and does not evade this.
Unweighted edges have combinatorial length \(1\).
A non-integer dilation is not an automorphism of a fixed simple graph.

If a three-dimensional metric is already expanding, the dilutions
\(a\propto\rho_S^{-1}\) and \(a\propto\rho_I^{-1/3}\) describe that
expansion.
They do not determine \(\ddot a\).
An independent \(\ell(t)\propto t^\alpha\) would give
\[
q=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha},\qquad
w=\frac{2-3\alpha}{3\alpha}
\]
for a single flat-universe fluid.
No earned equation produces \(\alpha\).
In particular \(w=-1\) is not implied by \(\dot\rho_S<0\).

On the chain, growth of \(\ell_W\) at fixed sheet lattice is
\[
a_\parallel=1,\qquad a_\perp\propto\ell_W.
\]
The Hubble anisotropy is then of order one throughout the woven window
\(\ell_W\ge 1.77\).
The kinematic point \(\ell_W\to 1\) erases the two-dimensional window and
lands on the overconnected value of Proposition~\ref{prop:over}.
You could regard that point as a three-dimensional substrate, in the sense
of Section~\ref{sec:h-j3w}, rather than as an equation of state.
