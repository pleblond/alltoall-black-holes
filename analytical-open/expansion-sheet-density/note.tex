\section{Sheet density tracks one axis}
\label{sec:h-rhos}

Take the three-volume to be the anisotropic ball of \eqref{eq:ds}, and
take \(\rho_S\) to be sheet area per that volume.
With the in-plane cell area held fixed, \(\rho_S\) scales as \(1/\ell_W\).
The identification \(a\propto\rho_S^{-1}\) then means \(a\propto\ell_W\),
which is \(a_\perp\).
The in-plane factor stays \(1\).

Under that identification
\(H=-\dot\rho_S/\rho_S\) is the transverse Hubble rate.
It vanishes while \(\lambda\) is fixed (Section~\ref{sec:h-de}).
A discrete deletion of stitches changes \(\lambda\) in steps.
After \(n\) such steps the transverse factor is
\(a_\perp(n)\propto\lambda_n^{-1/2}\), with \(a_\parallel\) unchanged.
A scalar fitted to the mean of the three principal radii moves when two
axes do not.
That scalar is a different object from the metric \eqref{eq:ds}.
