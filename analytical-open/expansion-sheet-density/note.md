# \(a\propto 1/\rho_S\) is one ruler, and it expands only one axis

If sheet density is area per emergent three-volume and the three-volume is
the anisotropic ball of `disorder-improves-isotropy/note.md`, then
\(\rho_S\) scales as \(1/\ell_W\) when in-plane cell area is held fixed and
the transverse spacing grows. Setting \(a\propto 1/\rho_S\) then means
\(a\propto\ell_W\), which is \(a_\perp\), not a scalar scale factor. The
in-plane scale factor stays \(1\).

\(H=-\dot\rho_S/\rho_S\) is the transverse Hubble rate under that
identification, and it is zero while \(\lambda\) is fixed
(`dark-energy-weave/note.md`). A measurement of in-plane versus transverse
arrival times as stitches are deleted is the entire content of this
hypothesis. Deleting stitches increases \(\ell_W\) in steps of a discrete
process; the prediction is \(a_\perp(n)\propto 1/\sqrt{\lambda_n}\) after
\(n\) deletions, with \(a_\parallel\) unchanged. A scalar \(a(t)\) fitted to
the mean of the three radii will move even though two axes did not, and
will invent an isotropic expansion the construction does not have.
